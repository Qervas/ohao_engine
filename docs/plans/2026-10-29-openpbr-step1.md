# OpenPBR step 1: the base layers in both renderers

**Spec:** `docs/specs/2026-10-29-openpbr-materials-design.md` (sections 2–8, step 1; rulings O1–O5 accepted by Frank,
2026-10-29: `Material` with OpenPBR's snake_case names, OpenPBR's defaults, the visibility buffer later, emission in the
engine's units, ReSTIR after this step).

**Goal:** both renderers evaluate one OpenPBR base (EON diffuse, GGX dielectric with IOR, `specular_weight` and
`specular_color`, F82-tint metal, energy compensation) from one Slang module. The path tracer becomes a correct estimator,
and closed forms hold both.

Every task ends with the engine running and every gate green. A task that moves goldens says which and why, and looks at
them before `--update`.

## Tasks

### T1. `shaders/material/openpbr.slang` and its CPU tests

- The module, a Slang module with no entry point:
  - `struct MaterialInputs` (the step's parameters, already resolved from textures);
  - `struct OpenPBRBase` built from it in a local shading frame;
  - `eval(wo, wi) → float3` (BSDF × cos), `sample(wo, u3) → {wi, weight, pdf, lobe}`, `pdf(wo, wi)`, `albedo(wo)`.
- The lobes:
  - **EON:** `base_diffuse_roughness`, with its analytic energy compensation (Portsmouth et al.) and its cosine/uniform
    mixture sampling.
  - **Specular:** GGX with α = `specular_roughness`², height-correlated Smith G2, bounded VNDF sampling (Eto–Tokuyoshi 2023).
  - **Dielectric Fresnel:** exact, from `specular_ior` modified by `specular_weight` (the spec's ε rule, clamped);
    `specular_color` on the reflection from above.
  - **Metal:** F82-tint (Kutz 2021).
  - **Energy:** Kulla–Conty compensation of GGX from tables `E(μ, α)` and `E_avg(α)` (metal), and `E(μ, α, η)` for the
    dielectric layer's albedo scaling of the diffuse beneath.
- **The tables:** `tools/material/openpbr_tables.cpp` (a CPU program on the module's C++ build) integrates them, 32×32
  (×16 IOR), by Monte Carlo with a fixed seed. They are written to `shaders/material/openpbr_tables.slang` as constants:
  generated, checked in, regenerated byte for byte by a check, as `gen_layout.py` is.
- **CPU build:** `slangc -target cpp` (`host-cpp` if `cpp` cannot) into `build/`, linked by `material_tests`
  (`tests/material/`, CTest `quick`):
  - `sample` against `eval/pdf`, χ² over a 16×32 hemisphere grid at 12 (roughness, view angle) pairs per lobe, p > 0.01;
  - `pdf` integrating to 1 within 1%;
  - reciprocity of `eval` to 1e-5;
  - `albedo(wo)` against a 2²⁰-sample integral within 0.5%;
  - **the furnace in the module:** a white dielectric (base 1, ior 1.5) and a white metal (base 1, `specular_color` 1)
    integrate to 1 within 1% at roughness 0 to 1 and view 0° to 85°;
  - the parameter defaults equal to OpenPBR 1.1.1's.
- No renderer changes; goldens unchanged.

### T2. `Material`, migration, the material table, the glTF reader

- **`Material`** (saved) replaces `MaterialOverride`:
  - `base_color` (0.8), `base_weight` (1), `base_metalness` (0), `base_diffuse_roughness` (0);
  - `specular_weight` (1), `specular_color` (1), `specular_roughness` (0.3), `specular_ior` (1.5);
  - `emission_luminance` (0, engine units, O4), `emission_color` (1);
  - plus the alpha fields and `subsurface` kept.
- **The codec migrates `MaterialOverride`:** `baseColor` → `base_color`, `roughness` → `specular_roughness` (0.5 written
  when the old file omitted it, so old files keep their look), `metallic` → `base_metalness`, `emissiveStrength` →
  `emission_luminance`. The checked-in scenes are re-saved through it. `schemaHash` moves in the CLI and serve
  expectations; the catalogue and schema goldens are regenerated.
- **`render::MaterialDesc`** carries the same fields and textures. `kMaterialRows` grows from 4 to 6:
  - row 4: `specular_color.rgb`, `specular_weight`;
  - row 5: `specular_ior`, `base_diffuse_roughness`, `base_weight`, and the KHR specular textures' indices packed.
- **The glTF material reader** (`ohao/scene/asset/gltf_materials.*`, simdjson):
  - the core metallic-roughness model (glTF's own defaults: roughness 1, metallic 1);
  - `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`, `KHR_materials_ior`, `KHR_materials_specular`, mapped as the KHR→OpenPBR mapping
    does;
  - the model loader takes materials from it for `.gltf`/`.glb`, Assimp's for other formats.
  - `W_LEVEL_UNSUPPORTED` stops naming these three.
- Renderers still read rows 0–3 as before: images unchanged. Goldens exact, except where the reader's glTF defaults differ
  from Assimp's (found, looked at, recorded).
- Tests: `scene_tests` (migration corpus cases, the defaults); a glTF fixture per extension in `tests/scene/fixtures/`
  read back field by field.

### T3. The path tracer in Slang on the module, a correct estimator

- `shaders/rt/pt/` replaces `pt_raygen.rgen`, `pt_raygen_offline.rgen`, `pt_raygen_realtime.rgen`, `pt_closesthit.rchit`,
  `pt_anyhit.rahit`, `pt_miss.rmiss`:
  - one ray generation shader with its modes as specialization constants;
  - a closest hit that returns the hit (position, geometric and shading normals, uv, instance, material);
  - the ray generation shader resolves `MaterialInputs` (`material/material_load.slang`, shared with T4).
- **The estimator:**
  - one path a sample;
  - a lobe chosen by albedo;
  - next-event estimation to lights (the emitter spheres and rectangles, the directional sun) and to the environment (its
    CDF), MIS by the power heuristic with BSDF sampling;
  - Russian roulette from bounce 3 by throughput, counter-seeded;
  - emission at every hit.

  NRD's and OIDN's auxiliary outputs (albedo, normal, hit distance) keep their meaning.
- **`ohao run --bounces N`** and `render.capture {bounces}` for the rt modes. `schemaHash` moves.
- Tests (GPU, `tests/renderer/check_material.py`, new, CTest `gpu`):
  - **white furnace:** a sphere under a constant environment of radiance 1 (the test writes a 2×1 Radiance `.hdr`), no
    lights; white dielectric and metal at roughness 0, 0.3, 0.7, 1 and diffuse roughness 0 and 1; the mean over the sphere
    and three view rings within 1%;
  - **the closed box:** six Lambertian walls (`specular_weight` 0), albedo 0.5, emitting 1, camera inside; radiance 2
    within 1% at `--bounces 64`, and `1 + ρ` at `--bounces 1`;
  - **`check_analytic.py`'s single-light cases grow:** rough and smooth dielectric and metal floors seen from 5°, 30°, 60°,
    90° against `tests/renderer/openpbr_ref.py` (a NumPy reference of the step's lobes, integrated numerically), within 3%.
- Every path-traced golden moves: looked at, recorded.

### T4. Deferred lighting in Slang on the module

- `deferred_lighting.frag` → `core/deferred_lighting.slang`. Analytic lights evaluate `OpenPBRBase.eval` exactly. Area
  lights keep the representative point (LTC in step 2).
- **Split sum:** the specular DFG from the module's own tables (per IOR, energy-compensated) in place of the Karis table.
  The prefiltered environment and GI's radiance keep their roles.
- **Diffuse from GI, the environment or the constant ambient** is weighted by the base's diffuse albedo
  (EON × the specular layer's transmission).
- **The G-buffer's sixth colour target** (RGBA8_UNORM): `specular_color.rgb`, `specular_weight / 2`. The IOR and
  `base_diffuse_roughness` go in the normal target's free bits (the plan's first act: list the bits).
- `vfx/transparent_mesh.slang` and `vfx/expand.slang` import the same module. The plane test (forward = deferred within
  1 LSB) holds.
- Tests: the furnace and the single-light cases in deferred (furnace within 2%), `vfx_gpu_test`, `light_cluster_gpu_test`.
  Every deferred golden moves: looked at, recorded.

### T5. GI's hits read the material

- `gi_trace.slang`'s hit loads the hit's material at its uv (`material_load.slang`): the bounce albedo is the BSDF's diffuse
  albedo toward the ray, not `InstanceGpu.giAlbedoPacked`; the hit's emission is added.
- `giAlbedoPacked` is deleted unless the plan finds another reader.
- Tests: the closed box in deferred, `1 + ρ` within 3% (its one bounce); `check_analytic`'s bounce cases;
  `check_fireflies`.

## Order and risk

- T1 first: everything stands on a module proven on the CPU.
- T2 changes data, not light.
- T3 before T4, so deferred is judged against a correct path tracer.
- The largest risk is T3, porting 4,312 lines of ray tracing GLSL with denoiser outputs. It is done as one new path tracer
  beside the old one until its tests pass, then the old one is deleted, in the same task.

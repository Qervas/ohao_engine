# Step 4b: jobs, waves, Jolt on jobs, the frame packet and the render thread

**Spec:** `docs/specs/2026-11-08-system-design.md`, sections 4.3 (declared accesses, waves), 4.5 (threads and the
frame), 5 (step 4) and 6 (the job system is our own). **Status:** planned 2026-11-18 on branch `step4b-jobs` (from
`2d048de5`). Frank's rulings: 4b is built (2026-11-17: "it's too crucial"), and the job system is our own work-stealing
task graph, with no enkiTS, no Taskflow and no new dependency (2026-11-18).

Today the engine runs on one unpinned thread of a 32-thread hybrid i9-13900HX. This step adds four things:
- one job pool shaped to that CPU;
- systems that declare what they touch, so the step runs in waves on the pool and gives the same `world.hash` at any
  thread count;
- Jolt on that same pool;
- an immutable frame packet, which a render thread records while the game thread goes on.

Each piece is written once, in its final shape, before a game is written against one thread.

## The requirement, questioned first

Step 4 deferred all of this on measurement (`docs/plans/2026-11-11-step4-frame.md`, "Not built"). Those numbers stand,
and each piece answers them:

| Piece | Step 4's measurement | What it serves, toward Call of Duty performance |
|---|---|---|
| Waves | Each system takes 0.0002 to 0.06 ms a step (`physics_bench`). | Not speed. Other people's agents write the games (requirement 2), so their systems are written against declared access from the start, and a wrong access is refused rather than left as a heisenbug. |
| `parallelFor` inside systems | 16 enemies take 0.058 ms; animation 0.001 ms. | A Call of Duty frame has tens of AI and hundreds of bodies, where per-entity work dominates. |
| Jolt on jobs | 1,000 boxes take 2.50 ms at 0 workers and 0.85 at 7 (Jolt's own pool). The blockout takes 0.009 ms at 0 and 0.034 at 7. | The pile is the one CPU cost over a millisecond, and a frame at 240 Hz is 4.2 ms. |
| The packet and the render thread | Recording takes 0.07 to 0.81 ms (`render_bench`, 100,000 instances). | Simulation and recording overlap instead of adding up, and the renderer stops reading the World. |
| The helpers on the pool | Each starts its own `std::thread`s. | No oversubscription: a cook at 32 threads beside Jolt's 31 is 63 threads on 32 CPUs. |

**No scene in the tree has enough work to show the gain.** Every step is under 0.1 ms except `physics_bench`'s
1,000-box pile. So 4b-0 builds **the crowd** first.
- **What it is:** the play scene with that pile, 64 enemies (AI, characters, animation, combat) and the player firing.
- **Where it runs:** as a `physics_bench` scenario (`crowd_step_ms`) and as a `render_bench` scene (`crowd_1080p`,
  written to `build/render_bench/crowd.scene.json` the way bench10k is).
- **Why it is a requirement:** without it, no measure can tell a working pool from a broken one.
- *Estimate:* about 3 ms of serial step (2.5 ms for the pile, plus 64/16 x 0.058 ms for the enemies).

## Where it starts

These are facts from the tree at `2d048de5` and from this machine.

- **The CPU:**
  - P-cores are CPUs 0-15: 8 cores with 2 threads each (`/sys/devices/cpu_core/cpus`).
  - E-cores are CPUs 16-31: 16 cores (`/sys/devices/cpu_atom/cpus`).
  - Nothing reads the topology, and nothing is pinned.
- **The step:** `World::simulate` (`ohao/ecs/world.cpp:423`) runs one total order: `SystemOrder`'s after and before,
  then the event edges, then names as ties.
  - The simulate systems are `physics`, `animation`, `combat` (the slice's, with the enemy AI inside), `vfx`,
    `audio_schedule`, and the sample's `shoot`. There are 10 sync systems, from `level` to `vfx_draw`. None declares
    an access.
  - 73 EnTT signal connections run side effects inside whichever system writes a component: `world.cpp` 9
    (`onTransformChanged` emplaces `TransformDirty`), `physics_scene.cpp` 26, `render_extract.cpp` 14,
    `instantiate.cpp` 12, `level_system.cpp` 6, `view_model.cpp` 4, `animation.cpp` 2.
- **Events:** `EventChannel::emit` (`ecs/events.hpp`) appends an event and takes the next global `seq` at once. From two
  threads, both a channel's order and the seqs would depend on timing. `World::defer` appends to a single vector.
- **Jolt 5.1.0:**
  - The backend makes its own `JPH::JobSystemThreadPool` (`jolt_backend.cpp:320`), switched by
    `BackendConfig::enableMultithreading`, which `PhysicsScene::ensureBackend` turns off (`physics_scene.cpp:209`).
  - Contact callbacks push under a mutex, in arrival order (`jolt_backend.cpp:409`). `drainContacts`
    (`physics_scene_step.cpp:7`) consumes them in that order, and a pair's begin point and normal come from its first
    manifold. At N threads that is nondeterministic, even though `publish` sorts the events afterwards.
- **The frame:** `LiveHost::run` (`runtime/live_host.cpp:133`) runs all of this on one thread:
  1. sleeps to the refresh grid;
  2. waits for the last frame's fence (`pollsLate`, while a player leads the look);
  3. polls, steps and syncs;
  4. runs `renderFrame`: `applySceneView`, `setHud`, `render()`.

  The sync systems write the retained `RenderScene` directly; `instantiate` calls `registerMesh`, which uploads and
  builds a BLAS. All submits go through `gpu::submit`/`submitAndWait` on one `VkQueue`. `gpu::reportFault` already
  handles a second thread that faults (`gpu_fault.cpp:110`, `quick_exit`). The logger is mutex-guarded.
- **Other threads:**
  - The RPC reader, the present waiter and miniaudio's.
  - Four fork-join helpers, each with its own threads: `cook/cook.cpp:85`, `asset/meshlets.cpp:94`,
    `runtime/image.cpp:279` (PNG) and `render/rt/path_tracer_pipeline.cpp:77`.
  - **There is no streamer thread.** Spec 4.5 says one "loads on threads of its own", but a load runs on the main
    thread: 40 ms of Sponza's 60 ms arrival (`docs/changes/2026-11-11-step5-assets.md`). The close corrects the spec.
- **Latency:** `feel_bench` (immediate, scale 1) gives a median look latency of 19.6 to 20.3 ms over 4 runs, with
  9.1 ms frames (`docs/notes/platform.md`). The budget is 15 ms.
- **Sanitizers:** GCC 16.2.1 compiles `-fsanitize=thread`, but the link fails: `/usr/lib64/libtsan.so.2.0.0` is
  missing. Neither `libtsan` nor `libasan` is installed, and there is no clang. (Checked on 2026-11-18 with a 3-line
  race in the scratchpad.)

## Global constraints

- **Determinism by construction:**
  - The same `world.hash` at 1 thread and at N, in any order within a wave.
  - One thread runs the same path (the waves, the staging, the chunking) without workers. It is not a second
    implementation.
  - Chunk boundaries depend only on the range and the grain. Reductions run in index order. No float is ever added
    atomically.
- **No new dependency, no fibers, no `sudo`.** The pool is `ohao/core/jobs`, at the bottom of
  `cmake/ohao_layers.cmake`.
- **Bounded:** every wait helps or sleeps; every spin is bounded and measured; no task blocks except in `Pool::wait`,
  which helps. No GPU work changes, so the goldens stay exact.
- **The fault path:** every submit and present runs on the one thread that owns the queue, through `gpu::submit`. A
  fence unsignalled after 2 s reports and exits 5 from whichever thread waits on it. Validation, its thread-safety
  checks included, stays at zero.
- **Rules live in code:** `tools/check.py` refuses `std::thread`, `std::jthread` and `std::async` outside
  `core/jobs`, `rpc/server.cpp` and `present_waiter.cpp`.
- **The machine:** full-load benches run bounded and headless, with `x86_pkg_temp` reported before and after. One
  visible window run at most; repeats are headless.
- **Budgets:** the crowd's budgets are added, with their measurement, after 4b-6. None is loosened.

## Design

### The pool

- **Workers by core class:**
  - The topology is read once, from `cpu_core` and `cpu_atom`. Without them, the CPU is one class.
  - The game thread takes CPU 0 and the render thread CPU 2, each a physical P-core.
  - The other 14 P-threads are P-workers and the 16 E-cores are E-workers. Each worker is pinned to its class's CPU
    set (`pthread_setaffinity_np`), so the kernel can still balance within a class.
  - The default is measured in 4b-1, on the crowd and on `boxes1k`. It compares a class set against single-CPU
    pinning, P-core hyperthreads on against off, and E-workers on frame tasks against background only. Step 4's
    unpinned Jolt pool was slower at 15 workers (0.92 ms) than at 7 (0.85).
- **Two priorities:**
  - *Frame:* the step, Jolt, `parallelFor`, and the render thread's work.
  - *Background:* cooks, PNG encoding and the RT pipeline compile, which run on workers only, E-cores first.

  The game and render threads help with frame tasks only, so a 5 s cook never lands inside a step.
- **Deques:**
  - One Chase-Lev deque per thread and priority (Chase and Lev, "Dynamic Circular Work-Stealing Deque", SPAA 2005),
    with the C11 orderings that Lê, Pop, Cohen and Zappa Nardelli proved correct ("Correct and Efficient
    Work-Stealing for Weak Memory Models", PPoPP 2013).
  - `take`: a relaxed store of `bottom`, a seq_cst fence, then a read of `top`.
  - `steal`: an acquire load of `top`, a seq_cst fence, an acquire load of `bottom`, then a seq_cst CAS.
  - The capacity is fixed at 4,096, with no growth. A full deque runs the task inline, which is always correct for
    fork-join, and it removes the buffer reclamation a growable deque needs. 4b-1 counts the overflows.
  - Threads outside the pool submit through one injection queue behind a mutex.
- **Sleep and wake:**
  - A worker spins for a bounded time, then sleeps on `std::atomic<uint32_t>::wait` (a futex). A submit wakes a
    sleeper only when no worker is spinning.
  - The spin (0, 50 or 200 us) and the cost of a wake are measured in 4b-1. *Estimate:* a futex wake costs 5 to
    20 us, about what a whole system costs today. So workers spin between a step's waves and sleep after the step.
- **Tasks and the graph:**
  - A task is a function pointer plus a 48-byte inline payload, from per-thread slabs: no `std::function` and no
    steady-state allocation.
  - It holds an atomic prerequisite count and up to 4 inline successors (more go to an overflow vector). The last
    prerequisite to finish pushes it onto its own thread's deque.
  - The API is `jobs::Graph` (`add`, `precede`, `run`, which returns a counter, and `wait(counter)`),
    `jobs::parallelFor(range, grain, fn)` and `jobs::background(fn)`.
  - Every task is named, for the profiler and the fault context.
- **Wait by helping:** a waiting thread runs its own deque, then steals frame tasks until its counter reaches 0.
  Since no task blocks anywhere else, nesting is bounded.
- **No fibers** (Gyrling, GDC 2015):
  - What fibers buy is a job that can wait mid-body without holding a worker. Naughty Dog needed that on six fixed
    PS4 cores, with waits throughout an existing engine; here every dependency is a graph edge.
  - What they cost: a stack per job in flight; `thread_local`s (EnTT's, the logger's, Jolt's) that are wrong across
    a switch; assembly or `ucontext` switches; and sanitizers, debuggers and profilers that lose the stack.
  - *Refutation tried:* "Jolt's `WaitForJobs` blocks in a task." It is called from the physics system, which is
    exclusive and runs on the game thread, and that wait helps.

### Declared accesses and waves

- **The declaration:** `SystemOrder` becomes `SystemDecl` (`ecs/world.hpp`, exported by `game/api.hpp`). It keeps
  `after`, `before` and `readsCues`, and gains:
  - `reads` and `writes`: component types (`ecs::types<Health, Transform>()`);
  - `resources`: named state that is not a component (`physics.scene`, `render.packet`, the audio schedule), each
    read or written;
  - `exclusive`: the system runs alone in its wave and may use the raw registry. Structural changes (create, destroy,
    emplace, remove) make a system exclusive, and so does calling a subsystem with its own registry use (physics,
    level, instantiate).

  Bevy's `Commands` (deferred structural commands) wait until a measurement shows two structural systems worth
  overlapping.
- **Signal effects join the writer:** the 73 connections become `World::onConstruct`, `onUpdate` and `onDestroy`, each
  declaring what its handler writes. A system's effective access is its declaration plus the effects of what it
  writes: writing `Transform` also writes `TransformDirty` and physics' placement queue.
- **Checked at install** (spec 4.3): a cycle throws, and so do two writers of one component or resource with no order
  between them, and a game system with no declaration.
- **Waves:**
  - A wave is a set of non-conflicting systems whose prerequisites all ran in earlier waves. Systems are placed
    greedily in the serial order, which stays the tie-break. Waves run in a fixed order.
  - A wave of one system runs inline. So does any system whose measured mean is under a threshold (from 4b-1's
    dispatch cost; *estimate:* 5 us), in serial order. Nothing inside a wave conflicts, so where a system runs cannot
    change the result.
- **Check mode** (`OHAO_ACCESS_CHECK=1`, on in Debug, set by hand in Release): a non-exclusive system reaches
  components only through `ecs::SystemContext` (`ctx.view<const A, B>()`, `get`, `patch`). Touching a type outside its
  declaration, or the raw `registry()` inside a wave, throws with the system's name.
- **Order fuzzing:** `OHAO_WAVE_ORDER=reverse` runs each wave's systems in reverse. A dependency the type check cannot
  see (a static, a context entry) then shows as a different hash.
- **Events:** inside a wave, `emit` and `defer` go to the system's staging buffer. At the wave's end the buffers are
  flushed in serial order, and only then are seqs taken. Every seq, and every event's place in its channel, equals
  the one-thread run's.
- **Taken:**
  - From Bevy's schedules: access sets, conflict-free parallel sets, exclusive systems.
  - From Unreal 5's `UE::Tasks`: prerequisites, priorities, named tasks (as in Insights), waiting by helping.
- **Not taken:**
  - Unreal's named threads, and free task-graph scheduling of gameplay: the fixed wave order is what makes the hash
    independent of threads.
  - DOTS's per-chunk write versions: they serve change filtering, and nothing here filters on change.

### Jolt on the pool

- **The adapter:** `physics/backend/jolt/jolt_jobs.{hpp,cpp}` defines `JoltJobs : JPH::JobSystemWithBarrier`, Jolt's
  interface for an outside job system.
  - `GetMaxConcurrency` returns the number of frame workers plus 1.
  - `CreateJob` takes from a `JPH::FixedSizeFreeList` of `cMaxPhysicsJobs`.
  - `QueueJob` and `QueueJobs` push frame tasks, and `FreeJob` frees.
  - Jolt counts its own dependencies, and the barrier's wait helps.
- **Determinism:**
  - Jolt documents its simulation as deterministic at any thread count, given the same calls in the same order. Its
    callbacks come from any thread, in any order.
  - So the backend sorts a step's contact events before `drainContacts`, by (body1, body2, sub-shape 1, sub-shape 2,
    type). `ContactEvent` gains the manifold's sub-shape ids.
  - Checked: `world.hash` after 600 steps of `boxes1k` and of the crowd, at 1, 2, 8, 16 and 30 workers, and
    `replay_test`.
- **Small worlds:** the blockout ran 4x slower at 7 workers. Below an active-body crossover measured in 4b-3
  (*estimate:* about 100 bodies), the adapter runs jobs inline. That is safe because Jolt's result does not depend on
  concurrency.
- **Deleted:** `enableMultithreading`, `workerThreads`, `JobSystemThreadPool`, and `ensureBackend`'s off switch.

### The frame packet and the render thread

- **The packet** (`render/frame_packet.hpp`) is everything the renderer reads of the world, immutable once published:
  - the view and the render settings (what `applySceneView` sets today);
  - the HUD, the lights and transient lights, the presented spheres, the vfx frame, the skinning palettes and the
    frame's dt;
  - the instance changes by slot: create, update and destroy.

  `render_extract` already writes only what is dirty, so 100,000 static instances cost nothing a frame. Slots are
  allocated on the game side; the allocator moves out of `RenderScene` into the packet's writer.
- **Double-buffered:** the game thread fills one packet while the render thread applies and records the other.
  Publishing is a swap and a notify. If the last packet has not been taken, the game thread waits, so at most one
  frame is ever queued.
- **The render thread owns the renderer, the `VkQueue` and the present:**
  - Every other caller goes through `RenderThread::run(fn)`, which runs `fn` on the render thread and waits for it.
    This covers a load's `registerMesh`, materials and textures, `SubmittedWork`, `render.settings`, `render.stats`,
    `captureFrame` and readbacks.
  - Captures stay synchronous. A load still blocks the game thread, as it does today; async loads with placeholders
    remain step 5's deferred item.
- **One thread is the same path:**
  - `ohao_game`, `ohao serve` and `ohao run` take `--render-thread on|off`. With it off, the packet is applied at
    once, on the calling thread.
  - Windows default to on, headless `serve` to off. So the goldens and `check_validation` go through the packet path,
    and the thread's proofs stay headless (`ohao run --render-thread on`).
- **Latency:**
  - The game thread sleeps to the refresh grid and waits for the previous frame's fence (`pollsLate`). It now reads
    that from a submitted-frame counter published by the render thread, behind the same 2 s fault wrapper. Then it
    polls, steps and publishes.
  - Poll to submit is today's time plus one handoff.
  - The gain is in a CPU-bound frame: an uncapped crowd frame becomes the larger of simulation and recording, not
    their sum. A play window waits on the GPU anyway, so it gains little, and it must lose no latency.
- **Faults:**
  - The render thread's faults report and `quick_exit(5)` while the game thread runs (`gpu_fault.cpp:110` already
    handles this).
  - `OHAO_INJECT_GPU_FAULT=lost:N` and `hang:N` must still exit within 2.5 s: step 1's done-when, run again.
- **Taken:**
  - The split between a game and a render thread with an immutable packet, from Destiny and Frostbite (Tatarchuk,
    GDC 2015; O'Donnell, GDC 2017).
  - Unreal's `ENQUEUE_RENDER_COMMAND`, as `run(fn)`.
- **Not taken:**
  - **Parallel recording into secondary command buffers.** The renderer is GPU-driven, so recording grows with the
    passes, not the instances: 0.36 ms at 10,000 instances, 0.81 ms at 100,000. Splitting 0.3 ms across threads saves
    less than the handoffs cost. It comes when a scene records over 2 ms.
  - **Unreal's RHI thread.** It adds a frame of latency, and the latency budget is already missed.

### The other threads

- **They stay:** the RPC reader (a blocking stdin read), the present waiter (`vkWaitForPresentKHR` under the
  swapchain's mutex) and miniaudio's callback. A blocking wait would hold a worker.
- **They move to the pool:** the four fork-join helpers, and a load's CPU work (naming sources and copying to staging,
  40 ms of Sponza's arrival) as `parallelFor` over its assets.

## The referee

- **Noise first:** each bench runs 3 alternated rounds after a warm-up, and its spread is recorded before any
  difference is called. `bench_env`'s noisy verdict reruns the bench.
- **The baseline** (4b-0, in `build/4b_base/`):
  - `physics_bench` (every scenario and the crowd) and `render_bench` (every scene and `crowd_1080p`);
  - `level_bench --only kit2k`, and `asset_bench` (Sponza's arrival, a first cook);
  - `feel_bench --modes immediate`, 3 bounded visible runs with the display awake;
  - the crowd's `world.hash` after 600 steps.
- **`replay_test`** (requirement 4, no new gate): the first process runs at `--threads 1` in natural order, and the
  fresh one at the default N with `OHAO_WAVE_ORDER=reverse`. Equal hashes at every checkpoint then prove the thread
  count and the order within a wave together, for about 0.1 s more.
- **Goldens and validation:**
  - The goldens stay exact through the packet path, and `check_validation` stays at zero.
  - By hand after 4b-7 and 4b-8: `OHAO_VALIDATION=sync` over `check_validation`'s scenes, and over
    `ohao run --render-thread on` on the play scene, the helmet and the path tracer. The layer's thread-safety checks
    catch a queue or pool used from two threads.
- **TSan, if `libtsan` is installed:** a `-fsanitize=thread` build of `ohao run` (the crowd at 30 threads) and of
  `replay_test`. Without it, the deque stress run, the access check and the order fuzzing stand in.

**4b-1. The pool.**
- **Build:** `ohao/core/jobs/{topology,deque,pool,graph}.{hpp,cpp}`, one pool per process, which the host configures

  - A by-hand stress run: 30 threads steal 10^8 tasks, each counted as run exactly once.

  - Planted: one wake in 1,000 dropped. It must show as a stalled `wait`; `job_bench`'s waits have a 2 s limit and

## Risks

- **A wrong declaration that the check cannot see** (a static, a subsystem's cache). The order fuzzing catches it when
  the hash changes. When the hash does not change, only TSan would find it, and TSan needs `libtsan`.
- **E-cores on the critical path.** An E-core runs at about half a P-core's speed (*estimate*), so the default worker
  set is measured, not assumed.
- **Wakes and spinning.** A step that wakes workers for every wave would be slower than serial; 4b-5 measures that
  against the inline rule and the spin within a step. Spinning heats a laptop, so it is bounded and the temperatures
  are reported.
- **A renderer call that misses `run(fn)`** is a data race. The Vulkan layer cannot see C++ state; the Debug
  owner-thread assert can.
- **Jolt's determinism** holds within one build. A Jolt upgrade is checked again at 1 thread and at N.
- **Latency.** If the render thread adds measured look latency, play windows default to off. The standard settles
  that; the budget is not loosened.

# Step 4: the frame

**Spec:** `docs/specs/2026-11-08-system-design.md`, 4.5, 4.6 and 5 (step 4). **Status:** planned 2026-11-11, after
step 5 (Frank, 2026-11-11: "go ahead with step 4").

Every pass of a frame is recorded through one frame graph: a pass says what it reads and writes, and the graph
places the barriers, begins the rendering and owns the memory. The parts of the spec's step 4 that no measurement
on this machine asks for are not built; each is named below with its numbers.

The graph and the one way to make an image come first, so that each pass is moved once, into its final shape:
its barriers, its render pass and its allocations change in the same commit.

## Where it starts

From two surveys of the tree at `cd96ed10` and runs on this machine (RTX 4060 Laptop, 2026-11-11).

- **The graph is a pass list over 5 textures.** `render/graph` (224 lines) keeps insertion order, tracks the layout
  of 5 imported images (reset to UNDEFINED each frame), knows no buffer and no storage access, and allocates
  nothing. Only the deferred renderer uses it, for 8 of its passes, 4 of which declare a part of what they touch
  and 2 nothing. Skinning, the scene upload, culling, vfx, SSS, the transparent pass, all of post, present,
  readback and the whole path-traced frame record outside it.
- **Barriers:** 117 `vkCmdPipelineBarrier` calls in 34 files, 9 file-local barrier helpers, one shared image that
  carries its layout (`rtc::Image`, 19 transitions in 4 files), 6 first-use flags, and elsewhere a convention
  (render-pass final layouts; an `oldLayout` typed on the belief that the last pass left SHADER_READ_ONLY).
- **Render passes:** 12 `VkRenderPass` and 12 framebuffer sites in 10 files; no dynamic rendering.
- **Memory:** 28 files call `vkAllocateMemory` (66 sites); VMA (`GpuAllocator`) serves the texture manager and the
  scene's buffers only. There are 5 image helpers and 10 files that create images inline.
- **What a frame's targets cost.** `render_bench`'s process, peak device memory (`nvidia-smi`): Cornell at 720p
  809 MiB, the play scene at 1080p 1,370 MiB deferred and 2,051 MiB path traced, with under 3 MB of content
  loaded. Between 720p and 1080p that is about 490 bytes a pixel. Nothing says which image holds what: the
  allocations are not VMA's.
- **Waits:** 47 `vkDeviceWaitIdle` sites: 22 at shutdown, 10 at a resize, 10 when a pass rebuilds a resource (6 of
  them reachable while a frame is being recorded), 2 at a load, 3 at a readback.
- **Pipelines:** 18 graphics sites without a pipeline cache, compute through one helper (14 files), ray tracing
  through one site with a disk cache. No shader hot reload: a shader edit builds in about 1 s and a new `ohao
  serve` shows a first frame in 0.23 s.
- **Descriptors:** 28 pools in 27 files, one set per frame slot rewritten at record time, layouts made from the
  generated bindings (`shader::layoutBindings`) in all but two files. The bindless texture set sits at set 0, 1
  or 2 by shader, and the path tracer keeps its own copy of the texture array.
- **One queue,** binary semaphores and fences, 3 frames in flight.
- **The CPU has nothing to give.**
  - Recording and submitting a frame (`render_bench`, medians): 0.07 ms path traced, 0.21 to 0.42 ms deferred,
    0.36 ms at 10,000 instances, 0.81 ms at 100,000.
  - Simulation (`physics_bench`, medians per step): the blockout 0.009 ms, the player 0.004, combat 0.002, 16
    enemies 0.058, animation 0.001, the pose sync 0.011, vfx 0.0002. The one millisecond is 1,000 boxes piling
    up: 2.35 ms.
  - The GPU is where a frame goes: 8.4 ms for the play scene at 1080p, 30.5 ms path traced.
- **Threads today:** the RPC reader, the present waiter, miniaudio's, and fork-join helpers that the cooker, the
  meshlet builder, the RT pipeline compile, the PNG encoder and the sky tables start and join themselves. Systems
  declare an order and no access; the sync systems (`instantiate`, `render_extract`, `vfx_draw`) write the
  `RenderScene` the passes read, on the one thread.

**The baseline** is `build/render_bench_step4_base.json` (every scene's passes), the five goldens, and
`check_validation` at zero messages.

## Decisions

Each by AGENTS.md's "Deciding".

- **The frame graph is built, over every pass.** The requirement is the roadmap's G2 and the spec's 4.6: a pass is
  added or moved without typing a barrier, and caustics step 2c's GPU pass waits for it. Today a pass placed after
  another is correct only if its author knew what layout the last one left.
  - *Taken from the state of the art* (Frostbite's FrameGraph, Unreal's RDG): passes declare accesses; the graph
    derives every barrier and every image's state; attachments come from the declaration.
  - *Not taken:* reordering by dependency and culling of unread passes. The frame's order is written once, in
    code, and a feature that is off does not add its pass; a second mechanism for either would be a second
    source of the same fact. Async compute: one queue, and no measurement that a second would overlap anything.
  - *Refuted:* "the validation gate already holds zero messages, so the barriers are right; leave them". They are
    right for today's order. The cost is in each change, and the path tracer's frame (13 barriers and 5 first-use
    flags in one 980-line function) is the worst place to add a pass, which is where 2c adds one.
- **No graph object: resources that carry their state, and a pass scope** (decided while building 4a-1). Since
  nothing is reordered or culled, nothing needs the whole frame before the first pass records. `gpu::Image` and
  `GpuBuffer` each keep what last touched them; `render::Pass` takes the declarations, derives the barriers from
  those states and records at once. No handles, no import step, no closures, no compile pass.
  - *Refuted:* "aliasing needs lifetimes, which need the whole frame". A pool that hands out an image at first
    use and takes it back after the last, called by the frame's code, needs no graph either (4a-6 decides).
  - The spec's word "frame graph" is corrected at the close.
- **One way to make a graphics pipeline** (`render::createGraphicsPipeline`, decided in 4a-1 rather than 4a-6):
  every raster pass's pipeline changes anyway (dynamic rendering names formats, not a render pass), so each is
  written once, through one helper. Bloom's three pipelines went from 130 lines to 25.
- **Declared usage, not flags.** A pass names a resource and one of a short list of usages (sampled in a fragment
  or compute shader, storage read or write, colour or depth attachment with its load and clear, transfer source
  or destination, a buffer as uniform, storage, indirect arguments, vertex, index or acceleration-structure
  input). The stage, access and layout come from the usage, as `synchronization2` barriers, one call per pass.
- **An image's state lasts across frames.** The graph keeps each registered image's last use; a history image is
  read next frame without a first-use flag. An image whose contents are not wanted says so (discard).
- **Dynamic rendering** replaces the 12 render passes and their framebuffers as each raster pass moves: the graph
  begins and ends the rendering from the declared attachments.
- **One way to make an image or a buffer** (`gpu::`, on VMA), named, and destroyed after the frames in flight
  instead of after a device wait. Every pass moves to it as it moves into the graph. This gives the memory
  breakdown that today's numbers lack.
- **What the target memory becomes is decided from that breakdown** (4a-6), not now: whether the renderer not in
  use keeps its images, which formats are wider than their content, and whether aliasing transient images inside
  a frame is worth its risk (a stale read is silent).
- **Not built, with the measurement that says so:**
  - *The job system, the systems' declared accesses and parallel waves.* Every system is 0.0002 to 0.06 ms a
    step; handing one to a worker costs about what it runs for. Built when a game's step is measured over a
    millisecond in systems that could overlap.
  - *The frame packet and the render thread.* Recording is 0.2 to 0.8 ms; a second thread would save at most
    that, and input is read after the wait for the previous frame either way. The packet has no reader without
    the thread. Built when recording is measured over a few milliseconds.
  - *Jolt on worker threads.* Measured (`physics_bench`, medians, Jolt's own pool): 1,000 boxes 2.50 ms at 0
    workers, 1.61 at 1, 1.06 at 3, 0.85 at 7, 0.92 at 15; but the blockout's step 0.009 ms at 0 and 0.034 at 7,
    16 enemies 0.062 and 0.090. Every scene that exists gets slower. `replay_test` passes at 7 workers; whether
    the hash is the same at 0 and at 7 was not compared. It is one flag (`BackendConfig::enableMultithreading`)
    when a game piles a thousand bodies.
  - *The transfer queue and timeline semaphores.* Step 5g's loads no longer wait; a scene arriving in a running
    engine costs 60 ms, 40 of it the main thread's CPU, which a second queue does not touch.
  - *Shader hot reload.* A new `ohao serve` is 0.23 s to a first frame after a 1 s shader build.
  - *One bindless table and generated parameter blocks.* The 28 pools cost nothing measured (0.2 to 0.4 ms of
    recording in all) and the binding numbers are already generated. A rewrite of every pass's descriptors for
    tidiness is not a requirement.
  - *One pipeline library.* Decided in 4a-6 from a measurement of what pipeline creation costs a first frame.

## Needs Frank

Nothing before the build. The step cuts the spec's 4b; that is reported, and reversed if Frank wants it built.

## The referee

- **The goldens:** the deferred scenes are exact, the path tracer to its floor. No part of this step changes a
  pixel; a golden that moves is a bug in the part.
- **`check_validation`** at zero messages over its scenes, and by hand `OHAO_VALIDATION=sync` over the play scene,
  the helmet and the path tracer after each part that moves passes: a missing barrier is a hazard it names.
- **`render_bench`** against the baseline, per pass, with the spread of three runs: no scene slower.
- **Planted:** for the rule that closes the step (no barrier outside the graph), a barrier typed in a pass, which
  `tools/check.py` must refuse.

## Parts

Each its own commit or few, the hooks green.

### 4a-1. The pass scope and the one allocator

- `render/graph/pass.{hpp,cpp}`: the usages, a pass with its declarations, `synchronization2` barriers batched
  per pass, dynamic rendering from declared attachments, the pass timer around the body.
- `gpu::Image` (`gpu/vulkan/gpu_image.hpp`) on `GpuAllocator`: named, with its views, its state per mip and
  layer, retired and destroyed after the frames in flight. `GpuBuffer` gains its state. The renderer owns one
  allocator and hands it to every pass.
- `render/graph/pipeline.{hpp,cpp}`: the graphics pipeline helper.
- Bloom is the first pass moved, as the pattern: one image with a state per mip, 15 passes, no render pass, no
  framebuffer, no raw allocation. The old `render_graph` stays until the passes it tracks have moved.
- **While passes are mixed:** a pass not yet moved reads a moved pass's image by the old convention (sampled
  layout). The moved pass ends with a `LEGACY(step 4a)` scope that leaves the image that way; each is deleted
  when its reader moves, and 4a-5 fails the build on any left.

*As built (2026-11-11).* Gates 6 of 6, the goldens exact.
- **Found:** `OHAO_VALIDATION=sync` over `check_validation`'s scenes reports a write-after-write in the play
  scene and the smoke doorway, deferred (an old-style barrier writing an image a render pass just wrote), on
  the commit before this part as on this one. The validation gate runs without synchronization validation, so
  it never saw it. It is in a pass not yet moved; the step ends with this check silent.
- `check_validation.py` takes `OHAO_VALIDATION` from the environment, so the synchronization run is one command.

### 4a-2. The deferred frame

Pass by pass, in frame order, each with its full declarations, its images from `gpu::`, and for a raster pass
dynamic rendering: skinning and the BLAS refit, the scene upload and the TLAS, culling and Hi-Z, the cascades, the
G-buffer, decals, the light clusters, emissive lights, RT direct light, RT GI, lighting, vfx, SSS, the
transparent pass. `rtc::Image` and its transitions go; the first-use flags go.

### 4a-3. Post and the way out

Bloom, TAA, exposure, the tonemap, the gizmo, the HUD, the capture preview, the blit to the swapchain and the
copy to staging; the swapchain image is a registered image like any other. The one-shot readbacks in
`vulkan_renderer.cpp` (20 barriers) use the graph or a single helper of it.

### 4a-4. The path tracer's frame

`PathTracer::render` becomes passes: emissive lights, the manifold gather, the cache update and resolve, the
caustics, the trace, the guide update, the denoiser, the copy out. DLSS and NRD record their own barriers: each
is a pass that declares the state it needs and the state it leaves.

### 4a-5. The rule

`tools/check.py` refuses `vkCmdPipelineBarrier`, `vkCreateRenderPass`, `vkCreateFramebuffer` and
`vkAllocateMemory` outside `render/graph` and `gpu/vulkan`, as it refuses a typed binding number. Planted and
caught. The device waits left are the shutdown's and the swapchain's.

### 4a-6. Memory and pipelines, from measurement

- The breakdown of a 1080p frame's memory by image, deferred and path traced; then whichever of these it
  supports: the idle renderer's images released, narrower formats where the content allows, aliasing.
- What pipeline creation costs a first frame; one creation path with a cache if it is worth one.

### 4a-7. The close

The change record; the spec's 4.5, 4.6 and step 4 as built; AGENTS.md's and ARCHITECTURE.md's "add a render
pass"; the roadmap.

## Done when

- No barrier, render pass, framebuffer or raw allocation is typed outside the graph and the allocator, and the
  build refuses one.
- The goldens are unchanged and validation is silent, synchronization validation included.
- `render_bench` is no slower on any scene.
- A frame's memory is known by image, and what was found is acted on or recorded.

## Spec corrections

Made in the spec at the close (4a-7), with these reasons:

- Step 4's "done when" asked that `replay_test` be identical at 1 and N threads and that the play scene's CPU
  frame drop with more cores. The play scene's CPU frame is under half a millisecond; there is nothing to drop.
- 4b (jobs, waves, Jolt on jobs, the frame packet, the render thread) is not built; see "Decisions".
- 4.6's reordering, culling, async compute, transfer queue, shader hot reload and single bindless table are not
  built; see "Decisions".

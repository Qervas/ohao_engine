# OpenPBR materials: one surface model for every renderer

**Status:** accepted (Frank, 2026-10-29, "fully accepted": the model, the steps and rulings O1–O5 as proposed). Plan for
step 1: `docs/plans/2026-10-29-openpbr-step1.md`. The engine takes the frontier option for its material model, OpenPBR
Surface, and both renderers implement it, so the path tracer can referee the deferred frame.

## 1. Why

The referee (`docs/changes/2026-10-29-referee.md`, `2026-10-29-gi-every-light.md`) measures deferred against the path
tracer, but the two do not implement the same surface:

| | Deferred (`brdf_ggx.glsl`, `clustered_lights.slang`) | Path tracer (`pt_raygen*.rgen`) |
|---|---|---|
| Diffuse | Burley | Lambert |
| Specular masking | height-correlated Smith | Schlick-k Smith |
| Fresnel | `fresnelSchlickRoughness` on analytic lights (an IBL approximation: a rough surface's grazing Fresnel held at 5%) | Schlick to 1 |
| Energy | Turquin polynomial `GGX_E` | none |
| Indirect specular | split sum | a heuristic: the mirror direction jittered by `cosine × roughness`, throughput 1 |

On a pure-specular floor at a grazing view they disagree by ×0.78 to ×3.1, and the path tracer's bounce on Cornell's rough
walls is grey light no surface model gives. Neither is "the" answer. Nor can either show what photoreal assets need:
coat (car paint, varnish), sheen (cloth), glass, iridescence, skin.

## 2. The model: OpenPBR Surface 1.1.1

OpenPBR Surface (Academy Software Foundation; v1.1.1, 2026-04-17; reference implementation in MaterialX,
`open_pbr_surface.mtlx`) is the frontier standard. It merges Autodesk Standard Surface and Adobe Standard Material, and
film tools are converging on it. We implement its definition, not an approximation chosen per renderer. A layered BSDF
(reflection and transmission; subsurface is volumetric), from the top:

- **Fuzz** (`fuzz_weight` 0, `fuzz_color` 1, `fuzz_roughness` 0.5): the Zeltner–Burley–Chiang LTC sheen (SIGGRAPH Talks 2022).
- **Coat** (`coat_weight` 0, `coat_color` 1, `coat_roughness` 0, `coat_roughness_anisotropy` 0, `coat_ior` 1.6,
  `coat_darkening` 1): a GGX dielectric with an absorbing medium. Layered by albedo scaling:
  `f = w·f_coat + lerp(1, T_coat·(1 − E_coat), w)·f_base`.
- **Thin film** (`thin_film_weight` 0, `thin_film_thickness` 0.5 µm, `thin_film_ior` 1.4): iridescence on the specular
  Fresnel.
- **Base**:
  - **Metal** (`base_metalness`): GGX with the F82-tint Fresnel (Kutz 2021). F₀ is `base_weight · base_color`, and the
    reflectance at μ̄ = 1/7 is `specular_color · F_Schlick(μ̄)`, all times `specular_weight`.
  - **Dielectric**: GGX specular with the Fresnel of `specular_ior` (1.5). `specular_weight` scales F₀ through a modified
    IOR (clamped so reflectance stays ≤ 1), and `specular_color` tints only the reflection from above. Below the specular
    layer, by albedo scaling, sits one of:
    - glossy diffuse: **EON**, the energy-preserving Oren–Nayar of `base_diffuse_roughness`, Portsmouth et al.,
      analytic compensation;
    - subsurface (`subsurface_*`);
    - translucent base (`transmission_*`: depth, scatter, dispersion).
  - Specular roughness is `specular_roughness` (0.3) with `specular_roughness_anisotropy`; α = roughness², as glTF.
- **Emission** (`emission_luminance`, `emission_color`), **geometry** (`geometry_opacity`, `geometry_thin_walled`,
  normals and tangents for base and coat).

**Energy:** single-scatter GGX loses energy at high roughness; OpenPBR's layering assumes lobes that preserve it. We
compensate GGX by Kulla–Conty/Turquin with directional-albedo tables `E(μ, α)` and their averages (per IOR for the
dielectric). The tables are generated by our own tool from the same Slang code (section 3), not fitted polynomials. A white
material in a white furnace returns exactly its input (section 7).

## 3. One source for every renderer

- **`shaders/material/openpbr.slang`**, a Slang module: `MaterialInputs` (the parameters, resolved from textures), and for
  each lobe and for the whole BSDF `eval(wo, wi)`, `sample(wo, u) → (wi, weight, pdf)`, `pdf(wo, wi)` and `albedo(wo)`
  (what layering and lobe selection need). Every consumer imports it: the path tracer, deferred lighting, the forward
  `transparent` pass, RT GI's hits, `vfx.expand`'s lit particles.
- **The path tracer moves to Slang** (ray generation, closest hit, any hit, miss: Slang targets the ray tracing stages). Its
  three ray generation shaders (4,312 lines of GLSL, the BRDF repeated in each) become one with its modes as
  specialization constants. **Deferred lighting moves to Slang** (`deferred_lighting.frag`, 406 lines, which today uses
  the GLSL BRDF while the forward pass uses a Slang copy).
- **Deleted:** `includes/brdf/brdf_common.glsl`, `brdf_ggx.glsl`, `includes/material/ggx_aniso.glsl`, the Slang copy in
  `clustered_lights.slang`, Burley, `fresnelSchlickRoughness` on analytic lights, `GGX_E`, the path tracer's dual-ray
  stages B and C and its skin oil-lobe tuning, and `gpu/vulkan/material*.cpp` if nothing uses them (to be checked in the
  plan).
- **CPU tests run the same code.** `slangc` 2025.11 (pinned) compiles a module to C++ (`-target cpp`/`host-cpp`), so a
  CPU suite links `openpbr.slang` and tests it directly (section 7). If that target cannot compile the module, the same
  tests run as a compute dispatch reading results back, and the plan says so.

## 4. Data

- **The component `Material`** replaces `MaterialOverride` (forward only), its fields OpenPBR's names
  (`base_color`, `base_metalness`, `specular_roughness`, ...), defaults OpenPBR's except where a ruling says otherwise
  (open ruling O2). Alpha (`alphaMode`, cutoff) and the screen-space `subsurface` strength (until step 4) stay.
  - The scene codec migrates old files: `baseColor` → `base_color`, `roughness` → `specular_roughness`, `metallic` →
    `base_metalness`, `emissiveStrength` → emission.
  - `schemaHash` moves.
- **The material table** grows from 4 rows to what each step needs (`kMaterialRows`). Every parameter is a factor and an
  optional texture, as glTF does.
- **glTF import:** a material reader of our own, simdjson, as `level/gltf_structure` and `anim/gltf_anim` already read
  glTF. Assimp keeps geometry. The core metallic-roughness model plus `KHR_materials_*`, mapped onto OpenPBR as the
  KHR/ASWF mapping does:

| Step | Extensions |
|---|---|
| 1 | `emissive_strength`, `ior`, `specular` |
| 2 | `clearcoat`, `sheen`, `anisotropy` |
| 3 | `transmission`, `volume`, `iridescence`, `dispersion`, `diffuse_transmission` |
| 4 | (subsurface: no ratified extension; scene files and `KHR_materials_volume_scatter` if ratified) |

  `W_LEVEL_UNSUPPORTED` stops listing the extensions we read.
- **The G-buffer** carries what deferred's per-pixel evaluation needs beyond today's (albedo, normal, roughness,
  metalness, AO, emission): `specular_weight`, `specular_ior`, `specular_color`, `base_diffuse_roughness` in step 1
  (one RGBA8 target); coat, fuzz and anisotropy's tangent in step 2. A G-buffer grows with every lobe. The frontier
  answer is a **visibility buffer** (instance and triangle per pixel, the material evaluated in a compute pass), which
  takes any material at a fixed cost. That is open ruling O3, not step 1.

## 5. The deferred frame

- **Analytic lights** (point, spot, directional) evaluate the BSDF exactly: their light is a delta, so `eval` is the
  answer. Area lights keep the representative point in step 1 and move to linearly transformed cosines (Heitz 2016) in
  step 2.
- **Environment and GI specular:** split sum, with a DFG table generated from `openpbr.slang`'s own specular (per IOR,
  energy-compensated), not the Karis table.
- **Diffuse from GI and the environment:** times EON's directional albedo, not a flat albedo.
- Whatever cannot be exact in real time is named in the code, and section 7's tests bound it.

## 6. The path tracer

A correct estimator of the same BSDF:

- One path per sample, no dual-ray.
- Lobes chosen in proportion to their albedo.
- GGX sampled from its visible normals, bounded (Eto–Tokuyoshi 2023).
- Next-event estimation to lights and the environment, combined with BSDF sampling by multiple importance sampling.
- Russian roulette, counter-seeded.
- Emission at every hit.

`--bounces N` on `ohao run` (rt modes) for the referee: deferred's one bounce against the path tracer's one bounce.

## 7. Tests (closed forms first)

- **CPU, the module itself** (`material_tests`, the C++ build of `openpbr.slang`):
  - every lobe's `sample` against its `eval / pdf` (χ² over the hemisphere, as PBRT and Mitsuba test);
  - `pdf` integrating to 1;
  - reciprocity;
  - `albedo(wo)` against a Monte Carlo integral of `eval`;
  - the parameter defaults against the published spec.
- **White furnace** (GPU, both renderers): a sphere under a uniform environment of radiance 1, no lights.
  - Every white, non-absorbing material (dielectric and metal, roughness 0 to 1, any diffuse roughness, coat on and off)
    returns 1.
  - Path tracer within 1%, deferred within 2% at every view angle.
- **The closed box:** inside a closed box of Lambertian walls (`specular_weight` 0, `base_diffuse_roughness` 0) of albedo
  ρ, emitting `Le`, every point's radiance is `Le / (1 − ρ)` (with `N` bounces, `Le (1 − ρ^(N+1)) / (1 − ρ)`).
  - Path tracer within 1%.
  - Deferred's one bounce at `Le (1 + ρ)`: this needs emission at GI's hits, which step 1 adds.
- **Single light at any angle:** `check_analytic.py` grows rough and smooth, dielectric and metal floors seen from 5° to
  90°, against a numerical integral of the definition (a Python reference of the step's lobes,
  `tests/renderer/openpbr_ref.py`). Both renderers within 3%.
- **The referee** (`check_parity.py`) is re-run at every step and its table goes in the change record.
- Goldens move once per step, looked at.

## 8. Steps

| Step | Builds | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `openpbr.slang` base: EON, GGX dielectric (IOR, `specular_weight`, `specular_color`), F82 metal, energy tables; `Material` and migration; glTF reader (core, `emissive_strength`, `ior`, `specular`); the path tracer and deferred lighting in Slang on it; GI hits read the BSDF's diffuse albedo and emission | CPU module tests, furnace, closed box (path tracer), single-light tests, referee table |
| 2 | Coat, fuzz, anisotropy (tangents in the G-buffer), LTC area lights; `clearcoat`, `sheen`, `anisotropy` | the same tests for the new lobes; a car-paint and a velvet scene in the goldens |
| 3 | Transmission (rough refraction), volume absorption, thin film, dispersion; the transparent pass on the BSDF; thin-walled | furnace with transmission; a glass scene against the path tracer |
| 4 | Subsurface: random-walk in the path tracer, a real-time diffusion profile in deferred, replacing the screen-space blur's `subsurface` strength | skin and marble against the path tracer |

Each step: engine running, every gate green, a change record.

## 9. Budgets

New passes and costs are measured and proposed per step for Frank's ruling, never assumed. Expected movers:
- `lighting` (a fuller BSDF per light);
- `gbuffer` (one more target in step 1);
- `rt.gi` (BSDF albedo at hits);
- the path tracer's inline capture in `serve_bench` (16 spp in 150 ms, Frank's ruling).

## 10. Rulings (accepted as proposed, 2026-10-29)

- **O1:** the component's name and field style: `Material` with OpenPBR's snake_case names (proposed), or camelCase as the
  other components.
- **O2:** defaults. OpenPBR's (`base_color` 0.8, `specular_roughness` 0.3) or today's (`roughness` 0.5). Proposed:
  OpenPBR's, so a default material is the standard's.
- **O3:** the visibility buffer, as a later sub-project, before step 2's G-buffer growth or after it.
- **O4:** emission units. OpenPBR gives luminance in nits; the engine's lights are radiometric in its own units, and
  physical units belong with the camera and exposure step. Proposed: `emission_luminance` read as radiance in the engine's
  units until then, the conversion named where it happens.
- **O5:** the many-lights architecture (ReSTIR, section 11): its own spec, after step 1.

## 11. Related: light transport at the frontier (not this spec)

The material model makes the referee true. What makes the realtime frame match it at a fixed cost is the light transport,
a separate sub-project for O5:
- **ReSTIR DI** (Bitterli et al. 2020; what UE5's MegaLights and NVIDIA's RTXDI build on) for direct light from any number
  of lights at a fixed per-pixel cost;
- **ReSTIR GI**, or a world-space radiance cache (SHaRC, neural radiance caching), for multi-bounce;
- a denoiser built for them (NRD ReLAX/ReBLUR, which the tree already links).

It would replace RT shadows' 4 lights a pixel, GI's per-hit light weighting and the 8 shadow channels.

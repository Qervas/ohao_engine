# Feel, step 2: the camera at render rate, and pacing

**Spec:** `docs/specs/2026-10-27-feel-design.md` ("Presentation-only look", "Present mode and late
input sampling", "Frame pacing", "Performance budgets", "As built (step 2)", rulings 7, 17, 19–22)
**Status:** done 2026-10-27 (plan `docs/plans/2026-10-27-feel.md`, Task 2), with one gate missed:
`look_latency_ms` passes (4.80 ms against 15); `frame_p99_over_median` fails (1.99 against 1.5),
its causes found and outside the engine, a ruling asked for (ruling 21)

## 1. Summary

The camera now turns on the frame after the mouse moves instead of a step and a half later. A
realtime window hands the World the look no step has consumed yet, and the interpolation draws the
player turned by it through the step's own arithmetic (`applyLook`), so the next step lands on
exactly the turn the screen already shows. The simulation, `world.hash`, saved files, snapshots,
replays, lockstep and headless captures and the goldens are unchanged. A play window also samples its
input late: it polls only when at most one frame is waiting to be presented. On the 240 Hz laptop
display, immediate present, the median look latency fell from **32.14 ms to 4.80 ms** (mailbox
32.07 → 4.81, fifo 53.24 → 7.62).

Pacing does not meet its budget. Two things make the slow 1% of frames, and neither is engine work:
the presentation engine holds the window's images for 330–500 ms several times a minute (in every
window, any scene, any present mode, with or without present wait; never headless), and a window
rendering at ~400 frames a second into a 240 Hz compositor gets its presents confirmed on the
compositor's ticks, which the late input wait then waits on. No fix was committed; ruling 21 lists
Frank's options.

## 4. The pacing investigation (no fix committed)

Every frame over 1.5 × median in the gated immediate run (891 of 23,672) was read with the frame
phases now in the CSV. The facts, each from a run:
- **Stalls, 330–500 ms (9 in the minute; 5–16 a minute across runs).** The CPU waits on a frame
  slot's fence; that frame's GPU work takes its usual 2–3 ms but starts 410–500 ms after the frame
  before ends (GPU timestamps, temporary logging). Before the stall about eight frames time out
  their 20 ms late-input wait; after it, presents confirm 30–110 ms late while the backlog drains.
  No device-memory allocation happens in the gap (VMA callbacks), and no other submission. It
  happens with present wait off, on the helmet scene as on the play scene, with no input at all,
  in `ohao_game` as in `feel_bench`, with either linked VMA copy and under another executable name.
  A 30,000-frame headless loop (render_bench, no swapchain) had no gap over 50 ms. The images are
  held by the presentation engine; the frame waits on its acquire semaphore. (A first comparison
  that seemed to show ohao_game free of stalls had run a binary built before the logging existed.)
- **The rest of the slow frames (4–5 ms against 2.50).** 834 of 891 spent the interval in the late
  input wait (median 4.6 ms, against 0.5 in normal frames): the wait for the previous present lands
  on the compositor's 240 Hz ticks. `--frames-ahead 0` measured 4.49 ms latency and p99/median 2.62
  (worse); `framesAhead` stays 1.
- The round's events (deaths, restarts, reloads) do not line up with the spikes.

## 5. Spec corrections and rulings

- The lead runs inside `interpolate`, not a separate `look_lead` system (ruling 19).
- The late input wait gives up after 20 ms (ruling 20).
- `frame_p99_over_median` missed, options listed for Frank (ruling 21): a borderless fullscreen /
  independent-flip window, pacing measured present to present, or a frame cap at the display rate.
- feel_bench's load probe leaves dwm.exe out (ruling 22).
- **Ruling 7, proposed:** `ohao_game` keeps `mailbox` as its default: the same latency as immediate in
  a window (4.81 against 4.80 ms) without tearing; fifo costs 2.8 ms more and paces worst.

## 7. Known gaps

- `frame_p99_over_median` fails (above); the stalls are visible as half-second freezes in any window.
- With a fast turn, RT shadows show noise on large flat walls for a few frames (their history follows
  motion); visible in the turn strip.
- fifo still settles into two-refresh frames and catches up after a missed refresh.

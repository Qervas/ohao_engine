The bounce the ambient term cannot know

Without RT GI the deferred pipeline's indirect diffuse light is a guess: the environment's irradiance when one is bound, and with none a constant, one push-constant scalar times the diffuse albedo (none for a metal) times AO (the baked AO, and SSAO when it is on). Neither can know that a red wall stands beside a white floor, so the floor stays white. `RTGITechnique` replaces that guess with traced rays, one bounce deep: light that leaves a lit surface, lands on another and is reflected once more toward the camera. With it the constant is not applied at all — the walls are lit by direct light and GI's rays only, as in the path tracer, which has no such term:

else if (!ibl) ambient += vec3(pc.ambientIntensity) * albedo * (1.0 - metallic) * ao * ssao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:359

On the helmet room that took the helmet against its left wall to 0.534 (the path tracer's 0.534) and the wall itself from 0.645 to 0.523 (the path tracer: 0.610, with every bounce, where RT GI traces one).

The result is a full-resolution `RGBA16F` image the lighting pass reads at binding 11: the incoming radiance over the cosine lobe in rgb (the receiver's albedo not applied) and the environment's visibility in alpha, the share of the rays that left the scene:

VkImage indirectLight; // RGBA16F, full resolution: rgb incoming radiance (no albedo), a the environment's visibilityohao/render/rt/render_technique.hpp:117

Two compute passes, no ray tracing pipeline

The technique is two compute passes, timed under their registry names: the trace, `rt.gi`, and in realtime quality the denoiser, `rt.gi.denoise`. Neither is a ray tracing pipeline. The trace uses inline ray queries — no shader binding table, no raygen, miss or hit shaders — and without `VK_KHR_ray_query` `init()` is never called and the frame has no indirect light.

// light for the lighting pass (binding 11), traced with inline ray queriesohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.hpp:4
if (m_rayQuery && m_rtGI->init(device, physicalDevice, m_width, m_height)) {ohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:164

Both are Slang: `trace` in `rt/gi_trace.slang`, and three entry points in `rt/gi_denoise.slang` — `temporal`, `atrous` and `upsample` — each with its own binding numbers and descriptor set layout. All four load into plain compute pipelines through the helper RT GI shares with RT shadows in `rt_compute.cpp`:

return rtc::createComputePipeline(m_device, "rt_gi_trace.slang.spv", "trace", traceSets,ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:269

The estimator, and where the albedo goes

For a Lambertian receiver of albedo $\rho$, outgoing radiance is

$$ L_o = \frac{\rho}{\pi}\int_{\Omega} L_i(\omega)\,\cos\theta \; d\omega $$

Drawing directions with density $p(\omega) = \cos\theta/\pi$ — a cosine-weighted hemisphere around the shading normal — makes the Monte-Carlo weight collapse:

$$ \hat{L}_o = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{(\rho/\pi)\,L_i(\omega_k)\cos\theta_k}{\cos\theta_k/\pi} \;=\; \rho \cdot \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} L_i(\omega_k) $$

with $N$ the ray count, $\omega_k$ the $k$-th sampled direction and $\theta_k$ its angle to the normal. That cancellation is why the receiver's $\pi$ and its cosine appear nowhere in the trace: it averages the radiance its rays bring back and writes that mean, the irradiance over $\pi$, without multiplying by anything, beside the share of its rays that escaped:

const float4 mean = float4(sum / float(rays), escapedSum / float(rays));shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:316

The directions are stratified in azimuth: ray $s$ of $N$ takes its angle from the $s$-th of $N$ equal sectors, jittered within it, and its radius from $\sqrt{u}$, which is what makes the projected disk sample cosine-distributed. With realtime's two rays a pixel, the pair always lands in opposite halves of the hemisphere.

const float phi = kTwoPi * (float(s) + rand01(key, s, 1u)) / float(rays);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:307

$\rho$ is applied at the very end, at full resolution, by the lighting pass, and it is the *diffuse* albedo, the G-buffer albedo times one minus metallic, so a fully metallic surface receives no diffuse bounce at all. The radiance itself still reaches it: the specular IBL reads it for what the reflection sees where the environment is occluded.

if (rtGI) ambient += gi.rgb * (albedo * (1.0 - metallic)) * ao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:358

Keeping albedo out of the half-resolution chain is deliberate. Everything the denoiser averages is incoming light, which varies smoothly across a surface; the albedo, which carries texture detail, never passes through a blur or a half-resolution sample.

What a ray brings back

A ray that hits a surface returns that surface's bounce albedo over $\pi$ times the direct light the hit point receives — its own one-bounce $L_i$:

return albedo * (1.0 / kPi) * directAt(at, -dir, key, s) * T;shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:264

The bounce albedo is not the hit's material. It is the RGB of `InstanceGpu::giAlbedoPacked`, loaded from the raw instance table at a byte offset the shared layout names, by the instance's custom index, which is its table slot:

const float3 albedo = asfloat(instances.Load3(slot * kInstanceGpuStride + kInstanceGpuGiAlbedoOffset));shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:262

The offset and the table's stride are constants of `shaders/shared/layout.json`, and the C++ side asserts both against the struct, so moving the field breaks the build rather than the bounce:

static_assert(sizeof(render::InstanceGpu) == shared::kInstanceGpuStride);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:17

The direct light at the hit comes from every light of the frame. The CPU writes them every frame from the `RenderScene`'s light list — in scene order, emissive proxies and dark lights out — into this frame slot's light buffer, which grows to the count, each with its share of the lights' power (the luminance of colour times intensity) in its fourth radiance component and its slot of Vose's alias table in the spot cone's two spare components:

lights[i].radiance[3] = static_cast<float>(power[i] / total);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:95

Each is attenuated as the lighting pass attenuates it: inverse square, windowed by the light's range, and a spot light's cone. A rectangle light is what it is from afar: a point at its centre of intensity `Le * area * cos(theta)` toward its face, a spot of cones 0 and 90 degrees (whose linear ramp is exactly the cosine), in the path tracer's units and with no range; a directional light is a direction, not a position.

atten = window * window / max(d2, 0.01);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:131
g.positionAndType = f4(d.position + 0.5f * (d.areaEdge1 + d.areaEdge2), 2.0f);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:63

The hit is shadowed with one ray. Each light would give the hit some luminance unshadowed; one is chosen with probability proportional to it, a shadow ray goes to it, and its light is divided by that probability. The mean is the sum of every light's shadowed light, for one ray where n would cost n. Up to `kGIWeighedLights` (16) lights, each is weighed, in one pass: a weighted reservoir, one random number rescaled at every light. Past that, weighing all of them at every hit costs too much (the benchmark scenes have 1,000), so `kGILightCandidates` (eight) candidates are drawn by power from the alias table, in constant time each, and one is chosen among them by what it gives. That is resampled importance sampling: the candidate's weight is what it gives over its probability, and the estimate, scaled by the weights' mean, is unbiased for any number of lights:

scale = sum / (float(kGILightCandidates) * luminance(lit));shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:228

Until 2026-10-29 the bounce saw only the four brightest lights. Cornell has twelve of equal intensity, so its walls bounced a third of their light. `check_analytic.py` now holds it: a spot aimed at a wall lights the floor only by the bounce, and 6, 12 or 20 spots at one place must bounce what one spot of their sum does, and twenty spread spots of twenty strengths what the twenty do alone, at reference quality (the realtime denoiser's clamp is not linear).

Before it, a bounce point in shadow bounced full light, and every surface facing a shadowed floor, an underside or a rim, glowed with light that was not there. One shortcut is left: the hit has no normal of its own — the trace has only the hit distance and the instance, not the triangle's shading normal — so it takes the negated ray direction, which is exact only for a ray striking the surface head-on.

A ray that commits nothing counts as escaped and samples the environment map in its direction, through the same equirect mapping the lighting pass uses, times `Environment.intensity` as the path tracer's miss applies it, or brings back black when none is bound:

return envMap.SampleLevel(envSampler, uv, 0.0).rgb * pc.cameraPos.w * T; // times Environment.intensityshaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:271

The sample is level 0 of the source image, which has a single mip level, so a miss reads full-resolution texels and a bright spot in the HDRI is as noisy a target as it is for the path tracer. The prefiltered environment the lighting pass reads is a separate image; the trace keeps the source so its misses match the path tracer's.

imgInfo.mipLevels = 1;ohao/gpu/vulkan/env_upload.cpp:85

Half resolution, and the guide

In realtime the trace runs at half resolution, rounded up, two rays a pixel: trace pixel $q$ shades full-resolution pixel $2q$, reading its position, normal and albedo from the G-buffer. Both are settings (`render.settings`' `rtGI`: resolution `half | full`, 1–32 rays); the shift that maps $q$ to its full-resolution pixel is pushed as a constant (1 at half, 0 at full), and the denoiser's reprojection and upsample take the same shift.

const uint2 full = min(reference ? pixel : pixel << pc.flags.w, pc.extent.zw - 1u);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:282
Why

The half-resolution reprojection keeps its original expression literally behind a branch instead of folding the shift into a multiply by 0.5 or 1.0: the folded form let the compiler contract differently and moved five deferred goldens by one LSB.

Alongside the noisy radiance it writes a guide word for the denoiser, packed as RT shadows pack theirs: the distance from the camera as a bfloat16 (a float's top 16 bits, never zero on a surface) and the octahedral normal at 8 + 8 bits. Zero means sky.

guideOut[pixel] = dist | (oct.x << 16u) | (oct.y << 24u);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:336

The half-resolution images — the trace's radiance, the guide, the two accumulations and an à-trous scratch image — stay in `GENERAL` for their whole life. The guide and both accumulations are ping-pong pairs, this frame writing one half while reading last frame's in the other.

The denoiser

`rt.gi.denoise` is four dispatches: one temporal pass, two à-trous iterations and an upsample.

**Temporal.** Each half-resolution pixel is reprojected by the G-buffer's velocity into last frame, and last frame's accumulations are read bilinearly over the four nearest samples — but a tap counts only if last frame's guide there is the same surface: within 10 % of this pixel's distance, with normals within $\cos^{-1} 0.9$. The weights of the taps that pass are renormalized, and less than 5 % of a tap in total is no history at all.

if (abs(gp.w - gc.w) > 0.1 * gc.w || dot(gp.xyz, gc.xyz) < 0.9) continue;shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:92

Two accumulations come through that reprojection. The short one blends the new sample with weight $1/n$, $n$ capped at `kGIFastHistoryFrames` (four); the long one the same with $n$ capped at `kGIHistoryFrames` (32). The two start and reproject together, so one frame count serves both: the short accumulation's alpha keeps it (capped at 32, the short blend capping its own use at four), and after a reset or a disocclusion the count restarts and the first frames average evenly. That frees the long accumulation's alpha for the visibility, which accumulates with the long blend.

const float n = fast.a; // min(frames of history + 1, kGIHistoryFrames)shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:163

The long accumulation is what the frame shows, and on its own it would trail any change in lighting by tens of frames. So before blending, its history is clamped to the range the short accumulation spans over the 3×3 neighbourhood — surface pixels only — and a moved or switched-off light drags at most a few frames behind:

const float3 slow = lerp(clamp(slowPrev.rgb, lo, hi), cur.rgb, 1.0 / n);shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:164

To read that neighbourhood, each 16×16 workgroup (`kGITile`) first computes the short accumulation for its pixels and a one-pixel apron into shared memory.

**À-trous.** Two iterations of a 5×5 B3-spline kernel over the long accumulation, at step 1 and then step 2, so the second reaches four pixels out and the pair together spans 13×13 half-resolution pixels, 26 across at full resolution. A neighbour's weight falls with its depth difference, against a tolerance of 3 % of the centre's distance that grows with the offset, and — only when the two packed normals differ — with the sixteenth power of their cosine.

const float invTol = 1.0 / (0.03 * gc.w * float(step));shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:194

The second iteration writes over the trace's radiance image, which nothing reads by then.

**Upsample.** Each full-resolution pixel takes the four nearest half-resolution samples, weighted bilinearly and by how well each matches the pixel's own depth (5 % of its distance) and normal (cosine to the eighth). Where no sample is the same surface — a sliver the half-resolution trace never landed on — it takes the closest match instead of a blur across the edge. The à-trous iterations and the upsample filter the visibility with the radiance, all four channels alike.

uOutput[pixel] = wsum > 1e-3 ? sum / wsum : best;shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:284

Every output depends only on its inputs — no atomics, no order — so a denoised frame is the same in every run that traced the same rays.

Reference quality

`RenderQuality::Reference` is the converged answer the realtime frame approximates. The same trace runs at full resolution with `kGIReferenceRays` (32) rays a pixel and no denoiser, and keeps the plain running mean of the reference frames since the last temporal reset in an `RGBA32F` image created on first use. The first of them takes its own value and reads nothing.

const float4 m = prev + (mean - prev) / (float(k) + 1.0);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:325

It writes the mean (radiance and visibility) straight to the output, and the renderer adds no `rt.gi.denoise` pass:

if (!m_rtGI->denoises()) giToLighting(); // reference: the trace is the outputohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:496

$k$ is not the renderer's frame index but the technique's own count of the reference frames in the mean, `m_refFrames`, pushed in `flags.z`. A temporal reset zeroes it, and so do a new image (the first reference frame, or one after a resize, whose contents the barrier discards) and any realtime frame, which breaks the run. A live session that switches from realtime to reference therefore starts a fresh mean instead of blending into whatever an earlier reference run left in the image — `sp2b_t10_a_reference_mean_restarts_after_realtime_frames` checks it, and RT shadows' means follow the same rule.

m_refFrames = 0; // a realtime frame breaks the reference runohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:350

Where the bleed lands

The lighting pass raises flag bit 3 when it holds a GI view and adds the radiance, times the diffuse albedo and AO, to the ambient term (above). The constant ambient term does not apply with it: GI's rays are the frame's only indirect diffuse light.

GI and image-based lighting are not simply additive. A GI ray that leaves the scene already brings back the environment, occluded by everything it passed; the lighting pass's diffuse IBL term is that same light unoccluded, so with GI in the frame it is dropped. The specular IBL stays, and GI decides what it sees: the prefiltered environment weighted by the visibility, and the radiance the rays that hit brought back for the rest (the lighting unit, "Image-based lighting"):

const vec3 incoming = prefiltered * gi.a + (rtGI ? max(gi.rgb - irradiance * gi.a, vec3(0.0)) : vec3(0.0));shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:383

SSAO multiplies the IBL terms and the constant ambient (when a frame without GI has it), never the GI image: a GI ray already stops at the occluder SSAO would be guessing at, so occluding its result again would count the same crease twice.

The handover is per frame and conditional on rays having been traced. Before the graph executes, the renderer clears the lighting pass's GI view; one lambda hands the output back, called from `rt.gi.denoise` in realtime and from `rt.gi` in reference, and only when `render()` dispatched — it returns early with no TLAS, no instance table, or, in realtime, no velocity view:

if (m_lightingPass && m_rtGI->rendered())ohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:463

The lighting pass, registered after both, reads that view at its own record time — once to raise flag bit 3, and once in its descriptor write, which picks the real view over the dummy fallback.

if (m_ssgiView != VK_NULL_HANDLE) m_params.flags |= 8; // SSGIohao/render/deferred/deferred_lighting_pass.cpp:183

A frame that traced nothing therefore renders with no indirect term rather than with a stale or never-written GI image.

Rays, seeds and cost

Every random number is an integer PCG hash of the render seed, the frame index since the last temporal reset and the pixel, hashed once per pixel, then of the ray and the dimension:

return pcg(pc.seed.x ^ pcg(pc.seed.y ^ pcg(pixel.x ^ pcg(pixel.y))));shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:57
pc.seed = u4(input.renderSeed, input.frameIndex, m_rays, lightCount);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:403

So the noise decorrelates from frame to frame — which is what lets the temporal accumulation converge — and two renders of the same world with the same seed trace the same rays. The history is dropped on frame 0 after a temporal reset (`resetTemporalState` zeroes the count and calls `resetHistory`), on a resize, and by any frame rendered in reference quality.

if (input.frameIndex == 0) resetHistory(); // a temporal reset: no historyohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:303

Realtime traces two rays per half-resolution pixel, half a ray per output pixel; reference traces 32 per output pixel. Measured with `render_bench` on an RTX 5070 Laptop GPU (2026-10-25), median of 100 frames: on `cornell_720p` `rt.gi` 0.064 ms and `rt.gi.denoise` 0.143 ms, within budgets of 0.30 and 0.20; on `helmet_game_720p` 0.070 and 0.151 ms; on `helmet_game_1080p` 0.164 and 0.339 ms. The denoiser costs about twice the trace at every size.

Sharp edges

The ray origin is pushed a flat 0.05 world units along the shading normal, with `TMin` 0.001 behind that, and the ray ends at 100 units:

const float3 origin = p + n * 0.05;shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:295

Both are absolute: an occluder nearer than the offset is invisible to the integral, a surface farther than 100 units counts as a miss and is replaced by the environment behind it, and both quietly assume a metre-ish scene scale.

Sky pixels are detected by testing all four channels of the G-buffer position for exact zero, in the trace and again in the upsample. The fourth channel is metallic, so a non-metallic surface passing exactly through the world origin gets no GI.

if (all(position == float4(0.0)) || position.w < 0.0) // sky, or a light's emitter mesh: no indirect lightshaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:285

A negative metallic is a light's emitter mesh, which the G-buffer writes as pure emission: nothing lights it, so it takes no GI either, and its guide reads as sky.

The trace queries with ray mask `kRayMaskScene`: every visible scene instance, neither hidden ones (mask 0) nor light emitter meshes, so a bounce never reaches a light's mesh and adds the light a second time (hybrid/visibility). The query is not forced opaque: a candidate from an alpha-tested or blended instance is committed only where `rt/alpha_test.slang` says it is hit, so a cutout bounces only where it has material and a blended surface bounces nothing.

RayQuery<RAY_FLAG_NONE> q;shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:246

The bounce albedo travels on `InstanceDesc` (default 0.73 grey); nothing derives it from the hit's material or texture, so a textured wall bounces one flat colour.

Key idea

The Monte-Carlo layer is textbook: cosine-weighted, stratified, unbiased for the hemisphere it samples. Every approximation lives in what a ray may know when it lands — one albedo per instance, one light shadowed per hit, a normal guessed from the ray — and every frame-to-frame artifact in what the denoiser keeps: a history clamped to four frames' range, tested per tap against depth and normal.

Contracts

  • The output is diffuse indirect light with the receiver's diffuse albedo (albedo × (1 − metallic)) applied. Any consumer of binding 11 must not multiply by albedo again, and one that also adds unoccluded diffuse IBL counts the environment twice.
  • The bounce albedo is `InstanceGpu::giAlbedoPacked.rgb`, read at `kInstanceGpuGiAlbedoOffset` in the instance at the TLAS custom index, which is the `RenderScene` instance slot. The layout constants and the C++ struct are tied by `static_assert`s in `rt_gi_technique.cpp`; change one, change the other.
  • Realtime needs the G-buffer velocity view: `render()` returns without tracing when it is null, and then nothing is handed to lighting. `denoise()` must follow `render()` in the same frame and does nothing unless `denoises()`.
  • `setSSGITexture` must run after the per-frame reset and before the lighting pass records, and only when `rendered()` is true — from `rt.gi.denoise`'s callback in realtime, from `rt.gi`'s in reference, both registered before `lighting`.
  • Each pass's descriptor sets are written at record time into the sets for the caller's `frameSlot`, one of `MAX_FRAMES_IN_FLIGHT`, and the slot's light buffer is rewritten in `render()`. Both hold only while the caller passes the frame-ring slot whose fence it has just waited on.
  • `GIInput::frameIndex` and `GIInput::renderSeed` are the only sources of sample variation, and frame 0 takes no history. A caller that passes a constant non-zero frame index freezes the noise in screen space.
  • Reference quality's running mean holds only the reference frames in an unbroken run: a temporal reset, a new image (a resize) and any realtime frame start it again. The shader trusts `flags.z` alone; anything else that writes `m_refMean` must zero `m_refFrames`.

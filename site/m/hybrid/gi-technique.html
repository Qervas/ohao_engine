The bounce the ambient term cannot know

The deferred pipeline's ambient term is one push-constant scalar times albedo times AO (the baked AO, and SSAO when it is on) —

vec3 ambient = vec3(pc.ambientIntensity) * albedo * ao * ssao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:297

plus, when an environment map is bound, two equirect samples of it, one standing in for diffuse irradiance and one for specular. Neither can know that a red wall stands beside a white floor, so the floor stays white. `RTGITechnique` replaces that guess with traced rays, one bounce deep: light that leaves a lit surface, lands on another and is reflected once more toward the camera.

The result is a full-resolution `RGBA16F` image of diffuse indirect light with the receiving surface's diffuse albedo already applied, which the lighting pass reads at binding 11:

VkImage indirectLight; // RGBA16F, full resolution: diffuse indirect light, albedo appliedohao/render/rt/render_technique.hpp:108

Two compute passes, no ray tracing pipeline

The technique is two compute passes, timed under their registry names: the trace, `rt.gi`, and in realtime quality the denoiser, `rt.gi.denoise`. Neither is a ray tracing pipeline. The trace uses inline ray queries — no shader binding table, no raygen, miss or hit shaders — and without `VK_KHR_ray_query` `init()` is never called and the frame has no indirect light.

// light for the lighting pass (binding 11), traced with inline ray queriesohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.hpp:4
if (m_rayQuery && m_rtGI->init(device, physicalDevice, m_width, m_height)) {ohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:167

Both are Slang: `trace` in `rt/gi_trace.slang`, and three entry points in `rt/gi_denoise.slang` — `temporal`, `atrous` and `upsample` — each with its own binding numbers and descriptor set layout. All four load into plain compute pipelines through the helper RT GI shares with RT shadows in `rt_compute.cpp`:

return rtc::createComputePipeline(m_device, "rt_gi_trace.slang.spv", "trace", m_traceSetLayout,ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:218

The estimator, and where the albedo goes

For a Lambertian receiver of albedo $\rho$, outgoing radiance is

$$ L_o = \frac{\rho}{\pi}\int_{\Omega} L_i(\omega)\,\cos\theta \; d\omega $$

Drawing directions with density $p(\omega) = \cos\theta/\pi$ — a cosine-weighted hemisphere around the shading normal — makes the Monte-Carlo weight collapse:

$$ \hat{L}_o = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{(\rho/\pi)\,L_i(\omega_k)\cos\theta_k}{\cos\theta_k/\pi} \;=\; \rho \cdot \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} L_i(\omega_k) $$

with $N$ the ray count, $\omega_k$ the $k$-th sampled direction and $\theta_k$ its angle to the normal. That cancellation is why the receiver's $\pi$ and its cosine appear nowhere in the trace: it averages the radiance its rays bring back and writes that mean, the irradiance over $\pi$, without multiplying by anything.

const float3 mean = sum / float(rays);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:175

The directions are stratified in azimuth: ray $s$ of $N$ takes its angle from the $s$-th of $N$ equal sectors, jittered within it, and its radius from $\sqrt{u}$, which is what makes the projected disk sample cosine-distributed. With realtime's two rays a pixel, the pair always lands in opposite halves of the hemisphere.

const float phi = kTwoPi * (float(s) + rand01(key, s, 1u)) / float(rays);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:170

$\rho$ is applied at the very end, at full resolution, by the pass that writes the output — the upsample in realtime, the trace itself in reference — and it is the *diffuse* albedo, the G-buffer albedo times one minus metallic, so a fully metallic surface receives no diffuse bounce at all:

const float3 diffuse = uAlbedo.Load(int3(pixel, 0)).rgb * (1.0 - position.w);shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:280

Keeping albedo out of the half-resolution chain is deliberate. Everything the denoiser averages is incoming light, which varies smoothly across a surface; the albedo, which carries texture detail, never passes through a blur or a half-resolution sample.

What a ray brings back

A ray that hits a surface returns that surface's bounce albedo over $\pi$ times the direct light the hit point receives — its own one-bounce $L_i$:

return albedo * (1.0 / kPi) * directAt(hit, -dir);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:129

The bounce albedo is not the hit's material. It is the RGB of `InstanceGpu::giAlbedoPacked`, loaded from the raw instance table at a byte offset the shared layout names, by the instance's custom index, which is its table slot:

const float3 albedo = asfloat(instances.Load3(slot * kInstanceGpuStride + kInstanceGpuGiAlbedoOffset));shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:127

The offset and the table's stride are constants of `shaders/shared/layout.json`, and the C++ side asserts both against the struct, so moving the field breaks the build rather than the bounce:

static_assert(sizeof(render::InstanceGpu) == shared::kInstanceGpuStride);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:16

The direct light at the hit comes from the frame's brightest lights, at most `kGIMaxLights` (four). The CPU picks them every frame from the `RenderScene`'s light list — by intensity, ties by order, emissive proxies and dark lights out — and writes them into this frame slot's light buffer:

if (input.lights[i].kind != render::LightKind::EmissiveProxy && input.lights[i].intensity > 0.0f)ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:256

Each is attenuated as the lighting pass attenuates it: inverse square, windowed by the light's range, and a spot light's cone. A rectangle light is a point at its centre; a directional light is a direction, not a position.

atten = window * window / max(d2, 0.01);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:104
g.positionAndType = f4(d.position + 0.5f * (d.areaEdge1 + d.areaEdge2), 1.0f);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:54

Two shortcuts make that $L_i$ an estimate of an estimate. No shadow ray is traced from the hit to the light, so a bounce surface behind a wall still glows: indirect light leaks through geometry wherever a lit-looking surface is not in fact lit. And the hit has no normal of its own — the trace has only the hit distance and the instance, not the triangle's shading normal — so it takes the negated ray direction, which is exact only for a ray striking the surface head-on.

A ray that commits nothing samples the environment map in its direction, through the same equirect mapping the lighting pass uses, or brings back black when none is bound:

return envMap.SampleLevel(envSampler, uv, 0.0).rgb;shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:135

The sample is level 0 because there is no other: the environment image the renderer uploads has a single mip level, so a miss reads full-resolution texels and a bright spot in the HDRI is as noisy a target as it is for the path tracer. A prefiltered environment would lower each ray's variance; none exists yet.

imgInfo.mipLevels = 1;ohao/gpu/vulkan/env_upload.cpp:64

Half resolution, and the guide

In realtime the trace runs at half resolution, rounded up, two rays a pixel: trace pixel $q$ shades full-resolution pixel $2q$, reading its position, normal and albedo from the G-buffer. Both are settings (`render.settings`' `rtGI`: resolution `half | full`, 1–32 rays); the shift that maps $q$ to its full-resolution pixel is pushed as a constant (1 at half, 0 at full), and the denoiser's reprojection and upsample take the same shift.

const uint2 full = min(reference ? pixel : pixel << pc.flags.w, pc.extent.zw - 1u);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:146
Why

The half-resolution reprojection keeps its original expression literally behind a branch instead of folding the shift into a multiply by 0.5 or 1.0: the folded form let the compiler contract differently and moved five deferred goldens by one LSB.

Alongside the noisy radiance it writes a guide word for the denoiser, packed as RT shadows pack theirs: the distance from the camera as a bfloat16 (a float's top 16 bits, never zero on a surface) and the octahedral normal at 8 + 8 bits. Zero means sky.

guideOut[pixel] = dist | (oct.x << 16u) | (oct.y << 24u);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:196

The half-resolution images — the trace's radiance, the guide, the two accumulations and an à-trous scratch image — stay in `GENERAL` for their whole life. The guide and both accumulations are ping-pong pairs, this frame writing one half while reading last frame's in the other.

The denoiser

`rt.gi.denoise` is four dispatches: one temporal pass, two à-trous iterations and an upsample.

**Temporal.** Each half-resolution pixel is reprojected by the G-buffer's velocity into last frame, and last frame's accumulations are read bilinearly over the four nearest samples — but a tap counts only if last frame's guide there is the same surface: within 10 % of this pixel's distance, with normals within $\cos^{-1} 0.9$. The weights of the taps that pass are renormalized, and less than 5 % of a tap in total is no history at all.

if (abs(gp.w - gc.w) > 0.1 * gc.w || dot(gp.xyz, gc.xyz) < 0.9) continue;shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:89

Two accumulations come through that reprojection. The short one blends the new sample with weight $1/n$, $n$ capped at `kGIFastHistoryFrames` (four); the long one the same with $n$ capped at `kGIHistoryFrames` (32). Each keeps its frame count in alpha, so after a reset or a disocclusion the count restarts and the first frames average evenly.

const float n = min(slowPrev.a + 1.0, float(kGIHistoryFrames));shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:159

The long accumulation is what the frame shows, and on its own it would trail any change in lighting by tens of frames. So before blending, its history is clamped to the range the short accumulation spans over the 3×3 neighbourhood — surface pixels only — and a moved or switched-off light drags at most a few frames behind:

const float3 slow = lerp(clamp(slowPrev.rgb, lo, hi), cur, 1.0 / n);shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:160

To read that neighbourhood, each 16×16 workgroup (`kGITile`) first computes the short accumulation for its pixels and a one-pixel apron into shared memory.

**À-trous.** Two iterations of a 5×5 B3-spline kernel over the long accumulation, at step 1 and then step 2, so the second reaches four pixels out and the pair together spans 13×13 half-resolution pixels, 26 across at full resolution. A neighbour's weight falls with its depth difference, against a tolerance of 3 % of the centre's distance that grows with the offset, and — only when the two packed normals differ — with the sixteenth power of their cosine.

const float invTol = 1.0 / (0.03 * gc.w * float(step));shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:189

The second iteration writes over the trace's radiance image, which nothing reads by then.

**Upsample.** Each full-resolution pixel takes the four nearest half-resolution samples, weighted bilinearly and by how well each matches the pixel's own depth (5 % of its distance) and normal (cosine to the eighth). Where no sample is the same surface — a sliver the half-resolution trace never landed on — it takes the closest match instead of a blur across the edge. Then it applies the diffuse albedo and writes the output.

const float3 radiance = wsum > 1e-3 ? sum / wsum : best;shaders/rt/gi_denoise.slang:279

Every output depends only on its inputs — no atomics, no order — so a denoised frame is the same in every run that traced the same rays.

Reference quality

`RenderQuality::Reference` is the converged answer the realtime frame approximates. The same trace runs at full resolution with `kGIReferenceRays` (32) rays a pixel and no denoiser, and keeps the plain running mean of the reference frames since the last temporal reset in an `RGBA32F` image created on first use. The first of them takes its own value and reads nothing.

const float3 m = prev + (mean - prev) / (float(k) + 1.0);shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:184

It writes the mean times the diffuse albedo straight to the output, and the renderer adds no `rt.gi.denoise` pass:

if (!m_rtGI->denoises()) giToLighting(); // reference: the trace is the outputohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:446

$k$ is not the renderer's frame index but the technique's own count of the reference frames in the mean, `m_refFrames`, pushed in `flags.z`. A temporal reset zeroes it, and so do a new image (the first reference frame, or one after a resize, whose contents the barrier discards) and any realtime frame, which breaks the run. A live session that switches from realtime to reference therefore starts a fresh mean instead of blending into whatever an earlier reference run left in the image — `sp2b_t10_a_reference_mean_restarts_after_realtime_frames` checks it, and RT shadows' means follow the same rule.

m_refFrames = 0; // a realtime frame breaks the reference runohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:278

Where the bleed lands

The lighting pass raises flag bit 3 when it holds a GI view and adds the image, times AO, to the ambient term:

ambient += ssgiColor * ao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:304

There is no `albedo` factor there: the GI pass applied the diffuse albedo already, and a second multiply would square the surface colour. The constant ambient term above still applies on top of it.

GI and image-based lighting are not simply additive. A GI ray that leaves the scene already brings back the environment, occluded by everything it passed; the lighting pass's diffuse IBL term is that same light unoccluded. So when GI is in the frame the diffuse IBL term is dropped and only specular IBL remains:

ambient += (specularAmbient + (rtGI ? vec3(0.0) : diffuseAmbient)) * ao * ssao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:348

SSAO multiplies those terms and the constant ambient, never the GI image: a GI ray already stops at the occluder SSAO would be guessing at, so occluding its result again would count the same crease twice.

The handover is per frame and conditional on rays having been traced. Before the graph executes, the renderer clears the lighting pass's GI view; one lambda hands the output back, called from `rt.gi.denoise` in realtime and from `rt.gi` in reference, and only when `render()` dispatched — it returns early with no TLAS, no instance table, or, in realtime, no velocity view:

if (m_lightingPass && m_rtGI->rendered())ohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:417

The lighting pass, registered after both, reads that view at its own record time — once to raise flag bit 3, and once in its descriptor write, which picks the real view over the dummy fallback.

if (m_ssgiView != VK_NULL_HANDLE) m_params.flags |= 8; // SSGIohao/render/deferred/deferred_lighting_pass.cpp:184

A frame that traced nothing therefore renders with no indirect term rather than with a stale or never-written GI image.

Rays, seeds and cost

Every random number is an integer PCG hash of the render seed, the frame index since the last temporal reset and the pixel, hashed once per pixel, then of the ray and the dimension:

return pcg(pc.seed.x ^ pcg(pc.seed.y ^ pcg(pixel.x ^ pcg(pixel.y))));shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:51
pc.seed = u4(input.renderSeed, input.frameIndex, m_rays, lightCount);ohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:330

So the noise decorrelates from frame to frame — which is what lets the temporal accumulation converge — and two renders of the same world with the same seed trace the same rays. The history is dropped on frame 0 after a temporal reset (`resetTemporalState` zeroes the count and calls `resetHistory`), on a resize, and by any frame rendered in reference quality.

if (input.frameIndex == 0) resetHistory(); // a temporal reset: no historyohao/render/rt/rt_gi_technique.cpp:251

Realtime traces two rays per half-resolution pixel, half a ray per output pixel; reference traces 32 per output pixel. Measured with `render_bench` on an RTX 5070 Laptop GPU (2026-10-25), median of 100 frames: on `cornell_720p` `rt.gi` 0.064 ms and `rt.gi.denoise` 0.143 ms, within budgets of 0.30 and 0.20; on `helmet_game_720p` 0.070 and 0.151 ms; on `helmet_game_1080p` 0.164 and 0.339 ms. The denoiser costs about twice the trace at every size.

Sharp edges

The ray origin is pushed a flat 0.05 world units along the shading normal, with `TMin` 0.001 behind that, and the ray ends at 100 units:

const float3 origin = p + n * 0.05;shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:159

Both are absolute: an occluder nearer than the offset is invisible to the integral, a surface farther than 100 units counts as a miss and is replaced by the environment behind it, and both quietly assume a metre-ish scene scale.

Sky pixels are detected by testing all four channels of the G-buffer position for exact zero, in the trace and again in the upsample. The fourth channel is metallic, so a non-metallic surface passing exactly through the world origin gets no GI.

if (all(position == float4(0.0))) // sky: no indirect lightshaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:149

The trace queries with ray mask `0x01`, named for static geometry. The `RenderScene` gives every live, visible TLAS instance mask `0xFF` and a hidden or released one zero, so the mask keeps hidden geometry out of the bounce and distinguishes nothing else. The query is forced opaque, so an alpha-masked surface is a solid bounce surface.

RayQuery<RAY_FLAG_FORCE_OPAQUE> q;shaders/rt/gi_trace.slang:121

The bounce albedo travels on `InstanceDesc` (default 0.73 grey); nothing derives it from the hit's material or texture, so a textured wall bounces one flat colour.

Key idea

The Monte-Carlo layer is textbook: cosine-weighted, stratified, unbiased for the hemisphere it samples. Every approximation lives in what a ray may know when it lands — one albedo per instance, the four brightest lights with no shadow ray, a normal guessed from the ray — and every frame-to-frame artifact in what the denoiser keeps: a history clamped to four frames' range, tested per tap against depth and normal.

Contracts

  • The output is diffuse indirect light with the receiver's diffuse albedo (albedo × (1 − metallic)) applied. Any consumer of binding 11 must not multiply by albedo again, and one that also adds unoccluded diffuse IBL counts the environment twice.
  • The bounce albedo is `InstanceGpu::giAlbedoPacked.rgb`, read at `kInstanceGpuGiAlbedoOffset` in the instance at the TLAS custom index, which is the `RenderScene` instance slot. The layout constants and the C++ struct are tied by `static_assert`s in `rt_gi_technique.cpp`; change one, change the other.
  • Realtime needs the G-buffer velocity view: `render()` returns without tracing when it is null, and then nothing is handed to lighting. `denoise()` must follow `render()` in the same frame and does nothing unless `denoises()`.
  • `setSSGITexture` must run after the per-frame reset and before the lighting pass records, and only when `rendered()` is true — from `rt.gi.denoise`'s callback in realtime, from `rt.gi`'s in reference, both registered before `lighting`.
  • Each pass's descriptor sets are written at record time into the sets for the caller's `frameSlot`, one of `MAX_FRAMES_IN_FLIGHT`, and the slot's light buffer is rewritten in `render()`. Both hold only while the caller passes the frame-ring slot whose fence it has just waited on.
  • `GIInput::frameIndex` and `GIInput::renderSeed` are the only sources of sample variation, and frame 0 takes no history. A caller that passes a constant non-zero frame index freezes the noise in screen space.
  • Reference quality's running mean holds only the reference frames in an unbroken run: a temporal reset, a new image (a resize) and any realtime frame start it again. The shader trusts `flags.z` alone; anything else that writes `m_refMean` must zero `m_refFrames`.

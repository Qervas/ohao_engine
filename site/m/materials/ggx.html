One GGX for every renderer

The isotropic GGX distribution exists once in the shaders, as `ggxD` in `material/openpbr.slang`, the specular lobe of OpenPBR's base. Every renderer that shades a surface imports that module: the path tracer (`rt/path_tracer.slang`, both RT profiles), deferred lighting (`core/deferred_lighting.slang`, through `includes/lighting/clustered_lights.slang`), the forward transparent pass and RT GI's hits. A highlight is the same function of the material in all of them.

import material.openpbr;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:17
import material.openpbr;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:30

The α convention

`OpenPBRBase` resolves the artist's perceptual roughness to α = roughness² in its constructor, and the Trowbridge-Reitz D squares α again:

$$ D(m)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\pi\big[(n\cdot m)^{2}(\alpha^{2}-1)+1\big]^{2}},\qquad \alpha=\text{roughness}^{2} $$

Here $m$ is the microfacet normal (the half-vector between view and light), $n$ the shading normal, and $\alpha$ the lobe width. $D$ is normalised so that $\int D(m)(n\cdot m)\,dm = 1$ over the hemisphere — the microfacets' projected areas add back up to the macrosurface.

alpha = rough * rough;shaders/material/openpbr.slang:423

The double squaring is why a painted roughness of 0.1 is already a near-mirror: α = 0.01, α² = 10⁻⁴. The module floors roughness at 0.01 (α = 10⁻⁴), a mirror at any renderer's resolution. The G-buffer stores roughness as authored and the path tracer's closest hit passes it through; the floor is the module's alone.

static const float kMinSpecularRoughness = 0.01;shaders/material/openpbr.slang:27

A denominator that does not cancel

The textbook denominator forms $\alpha^2 - 1$, which rounds to −1 in single precision at α = 10⁻⁴ (α² = 10⁻⁸), and the peak becomes 0/0. `ggxD` writes the same quantity in the microfacet normal's local components, $m_x^2+m_y^2+m_z^2\alpha^2$ — equal to $(n\cdot m)^2(\alpha^2-1)+1$ for a unit $m$ — and never forms the difference:

const float d = dot(m.xy, m.xy) + m.z * m.z * a2;shaders/material/openpbr.slang:118

So the function has no guard at all: nothing is added to the denominator, and it is exact Trowbridge-Reitz at whatever width it is handed. Its normalisation holds at every roughness down to the floor; the floor is the only thing that keeps D finite, and it does so by substituting a slightly wider — still correctly normalised — lobe rather than by removing the peak's mass.

Masking-shadowing: height-correlated

The single-scatter lobe is $D\,G_2/4\mu_o$ (times $\cos\omega_i$) with Heitz's height-correlated $G_2$, written from the Smith Λ function:

$$ G_2(\omega_o,\omega_i)=\frac{1}{1+\Lambda(\omega_o)+\Lambda(\omega_i)},\qquad \Lambda(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(-1+\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}\tan^{2}\theta}\right) $$

with $\omega_o$ the view direction, $\omega_i$ the light direction and $\mu_o$ the cosine of the view to the normal. The denominator is explicit, so there is no folded-in visibility term to divide twice.

const float G2 = 1.0 / (1.0 + ggxLambda(wo.z, a2) + ggxLambda(wi.z, a2));shaders/material/openpbr.slang:133

Deferred lighting evaluates the same `ggxSingle` through `OpenPBRBase.evalLobes`, so the rasteriser and the path tracer agree on masking-shadowing exactly; sphere and rectangle lights only widen the lobe, through `withRoughness`.

Fresnel: exact for dielectrics, F82-tint for metals

OpenPBR's base gives a dielectric the exact unpolarised Fresnel of its IOR (after `specular_weight` has scaled its F0) and a metal the F82-tint form: Schlick minus a $\mu(1-\mu)^6$ term fitted so the reflectance at $\mu = 1/7$ is `specular_color` times Schlick's:

return saturate(F0 + (1.0 - F0) * m5 - b * (c * m5 * m));shaders/material/openpbr.slang:215

Both the path tracer and deferred lighting call it, for every light and, through `specularAlbedo`, for the environment's split sum.

Why

A rough dielectric under single-scatter GGX with full Schlick gets a bright rim at grazing angles that nothing cancels. OpenPBR removes the cause: Kulla-Conty's multiple-scattering lobe returns the energy single-scatter GGX loses, and the diffuse beneath is scaled by what the specular lets through, so the white furnace holds with the exact Fresnel and the deferred silhouette matches the path tracer's.

return favg * favg * e1Avg / (1.0 - favg * (1.0 - e1Avg));shaders/material/openpbr.slang:221

Sampling: bounded VNDF with its exact density

The path tracer draws a microfacet normal from the visible normals, with Eto and Tokuyoshi's 2023 bound: the spherical cap it samples leaves out the normals whose reflection would fall below the horizon, so no sample is wasted under the surface. `ggxPdfBounded` is the density of the reflected direction under that sampling, and the mixture of it with the diffuse lobe's density is what the sampler reports:

s.pdf = ps * ggxPdfBounded(wo, s.wi, alpha) + (1.0 - ps) * eonPdf(wo, s.wi, diffRough);shaders/material/openpbr.slang:556

The estimator weight is the whole BSDF over that density, so the sampler and the evaluator cannot drift apart:

if (s.pdf > 0.0) s.weight = eval(wo, s.wi, t) / s.pdf;shaders/material/openpbr.slang:557
Key idea

D, its density and the BSDF the weight divides are one module. A change to `ggxD` moves the deferred frame, the path tracer, the transparent pass and RT GI together, and moves the energy tables with them: `openpbr_tables --check` fails until they are regenerated from the module.

No anisotropy

OpenPBR's base as built has no anisotropic roughness: one α per surface, one tangent-free lobe. A material's roughness texture scales that α and nothing else.

Contracts

  • `ggxD`, `ggxSingle`, the Fresnels and `ggxPdfBounded` are the one specular lobe of the path tracer, deferred lighting, the transparent pass and RT GI's hits. A change to `material/openpbr.slang` moves all four, and the energy tables (`tools/material/openpbr_tables`) must be regenerated from the module.
  • Roughness is floored once, in the module, at 0.01. A renderer that floors it again before handing it over renders a different material from the others.
  • α = roughness² everywhere: `OpenPBRBase`'s constructor, `ggxD`, `ggxLambda`, the sampler and its density. Changing the convention in one place biases the estimator silently.
  • The sampler's density (`ggxPdfBounded` mixed with `eonPdf`) is also what MIS weighs light samples against; it must stay the density the sampler actually draws from.

Two ways to find a light

From a shading point a path tracer can reach a light two ways: sample the light — a point on it, or a direction from the environment map's luminance CDF — and shadow-trace toward it, or sample the BSDF, let the bounce ray travel, and take what it meets. Both are unbiased estimators of the same integral, and each fails where the other works. Light sampling wastes nearly every sample on a smooth surface, where almost no light direction has meaningful BSDF response; BSDF sampling wastes nearly every sample under a small bright source, which almost no BSDF direction hits. Running both and adding counts the light twice.

Multiple importance sampling is the correction — scale each sample by how likely the *other* strategy was to have produced that same direction. OHAO's path tracer, `rt/path_tracer.slang`, which both RT profiles run, weighs every light with an emitter mesh and the environment against BSDF sampling with the power heuristic.

The power heuristic

$$ w_A(\omega) = \frac{p_A(\omega)^\beta}{p_A(\omega)^\beta + p_B(\omega)^\beta}, \qquad w_A(\omega) + w_B(\omega) = 1 $$

Here $p_A$ and $p_B$ are the two strategies' probability densities *evaluated at the same direction* $\omega$, both in solid-angle measure, and $\beta$ the exponent: $\beta = 1$ is Veach and Guibas' balance heuristic, $\beta = 2$ their power heuristic. The guarantee is the second equation: because the weights sum to one at every direction, the combined estimator stays unbiased no matter how badly the two densities disagree.

The path tracer writes the power heuristic itself, and returns exactly 0 when both densities are 0, so a direction neither strategy could produce contributes nothing rather than a NaN:

return a2 + b2 > 0.0 ? a2 / (a2 + b2) : 0.0;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:146
Why

Veach's result is that the balance heuristic's variance exceeds that of any other weighting by at most a small additive term, and that the power heuristic does better in the common case where one strategy's density dominates the other's — a sharp highlight under a large light, a small light over a rough surface. The path tracer is the image the deferred frame is judged by, and two extra multiplies a weight are not its cost.

The partition over every light

At every vertex the path tracer picks one light uniformly, so every light density it reports carries the choice, $1/n$:

const uint i = min(uint(rng.next() * float(n)), n - 1u);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:245

The choice and the light's point are the first three coordinates of one 4-D Sobol point (the vertex's light group, *Owen-scrambled Sobol*), so they are stratified together across a pixel's samples.

A sphere (a point or spot light, at least 1 cm) is sampled uniformly over its cone of directions, a rectangle by its area, a sun along its direction. The bounce rays that the BSDF draws are traced against the scene *and* the lights' emitter meshes — the camera ray against everything the camera sees:

const PathPayload hit = trace(origin, dir, depth == 0u ? kRayMaskCamera : (kRayMaskScene | kTlasMaskEmitter), 1.0e4);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:486

An emitter mesh carries its light's index in its instance row, and the closest hit returns it, so a bounce ray that lands on a light knows which light it found:

payload.light = uint(c.instanceRow9.w + 0.5);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:899

`emitterRadiance` then gives that light's radiance toward the ray's origin and the density light sampling had for the same direction, and the bounce's contribution is weighed against it with the BSDF density the ray was drawn with:

add = throughput * le * powerHeuristic(lastPdf, lightPdf);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:509

The light-sampling side mirrors it. A sun is a delta, so no bounce ray can find it and its sample is taken at full weight; a sphere or rectangle with an emitter mesh is weighed against the BSDF's density; a light *without* an emitter mesh is found only by light sampling, and takes full weight too:

// A sun is a delta (its radiance already over its choice); a light without anshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:630
const float3 c = throughput * f * ls.radiance * w;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:633

Which lights have a mesh is the scene's to say. `LightTable::setEmitterLights` receives the RT indices of the lights RenderScene made an emitter instance for, and the upload flags them in a spare word of each light — `extra.w` for a rectangle, `extra2.z` for a sphere:

if (rt[i].positionAndType.w > 2.5f) rt[i].extra.w = 1.0f; // a rectangleohao/render/scene/light_table.cpp:237
l.emitter = l.type == 3u ? e.w > 0.5 : (l.type != 1u && f.z > 0.5);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:191

The flag is load-bearing in both directions. A light weighed against BSDF sampling when no bounce ray can meet it loses the share the BSDF side would have carried; a light taken at full weight while its mesh is also found adds its light twice.

The environment is the same pair. A direction drawn from the map's CDF is weighed against the BSDF:

const float3 le = envRadiance(L) * powerHeuristic(pdfL, base.pdf(wo, wi, tables)) / pdfL;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:652

and a bounce ray that leaves the scene against the CDF — except the camera ray, which no light strategy could have produced:

const float w = depth == 0u ? 1.0 : powerHeuristic(lastPdf, envPdf(dir));shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:493

What is left out is emissive surfaces. They are found by BSDF sampling alone, at full weight:

add = throughput * hit.emission; // emissive surfaces: found by BSDF sampling onlyshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:515

and the RT light buffer does not hold the sphere proxies the raster frame makes for them, so nothing else finds them:

// meets the surfaces themselves, and a proxy light beside them would count their light twice.ohao/render/scene/light_table.cpp:177
Key idea

Every light with a mesh and the environment sit in a two-strategy partition, and every other source is found by exactly one strategy. A source found by both strategies without a weight would be counted twice; one found by neither would be dark.

The BSDF density on the other side of every one of those weights is the mixture the sampler actually draws from: `OpenPBRBase::sample` picks the specular lobe with probability `specularShare` and the diffuse lobe otherwise, and `pdf` reports the same mixture for any direction, so the density a bounce ray was drawn with and the density a light sample is weighed against are one function.

return ps * ggxPdfBounded(wo, wi, alpha) + (1.0 - ps) * eonPdf(wo, wi, diffRough);shaders/material/openpbr.slang:533

Both sides must be quoted in solid angle

A ratio of densities means nothing unless both are in the same measure. The env sampler draws from a 2D CDF built over equirectangular pixels — a density in UV space — and converts to solid angle by dividing out the Jacobian of the mapping:

$$ p_\Omega(\omega) = \frac{p_{uv}(u,v)}{2\pi^2 \sin\theta} $$

$p_{uv}$ is the luminance-proportional density over the normalised $(u,v)$ square, $\theta$ the polar angle, and $2\pi \cdot \pi \cdot \sin\theta$ the area stretch at that latitude — which is why the poles, where $\sin\theta \to 0$, need the `1e-4` floor before dividing:

return max(cond * marg * float(W) * float(H) / (kTwoPi * kPi * max(sin(theta), 1e-4)), 0.0);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:358

The light densities are in the same measure. A sphere's cone of directions has solid angle $2\pi(1-\cos\theta_{max})$, so a uniform direction in it has density $1/(2\pi(1-\cos\theta_{max}))$, computed as $\sin^2/(1+\sqrt{1-\sin^2})$ so that a distant small sphere does not cancel to 0:

return 1.0 / (kTwoPi * oneMinusCos);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:221

A rectangle is sampled uniformly by area, $1/A$, and turned into solid angle by $d^2/\cos\theta_L$, the light-side cosine:

s.pdf = choose * d2 / (cosL * l.area);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:268

The BSDF side's environment density is evaluated by the raygen itself: the miss shader only clears the payload's hit flag, and the escaped bounce ray calls `envPdf(dir)`, the same function the light-sampling side uses, so both halves of the weight are one density.

No delta lobe

The power heuristic assumes both strategies have finite densities; a mirror lobe's is a Dirac delta. The path tracer has no delta lobe: its GGX is never sharper than roughness 0.01 — alpha $10^{-4}$, a mirror at any renderer's resolution — so every density it produces is finite and mirrors are weighed like any other surface, with no threshold at which a light changes strategy.

static const float kMinSpecularRoughness = 0.01;shaders/material/openpbr.slang:27

Contracts

  • Both densities must be in solid-angle measure. A producer returning UV or area density rebalances the weights instead of failing.
  • Argument order encodes which strategy is weighted: the first density's strategy gets the weight. Swapping the arguments inverts the partition, and the symptom is a brightness shift, not a crash.
  • A light's emitter flag (`LightTable::setEmitterLights`) must match whether a bounce ray can meet its mesh. A flagged light without a mesh loses energy; an unflagged light with one counts twice.
  • A bounce ray's density (`lastPdf`) and a light sample's BSDF density must both be `OpenPBRBase::pdf`, the mixture the sampler draws from.
  • The environment's two sides must both call `envPdf`; a second environment strategy must report its density through it.

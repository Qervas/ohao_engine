The decision that shapes the rest: position lives in the buffer

`gbuffer.frag` writes seven colour outputs plus depth, and the first one settles the character of the whole pipeline: it stores the interpolated **world position**, verbatim, with metallic in alpha.

outGBuffer0 = vec4(fragWorldPos, metallic);shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:209

The lighting pass, `core/deferred_lighting.slang`, reads it straight back and never touches the depth buffer.

const float3 P = g0.rgb;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:171

That target is `R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT` — IEEE binary16, an 11-bit significand. In the binade $[2^e, 2^{e+1})$ the spacing between representable values is

$$ \mathrm{ulp}(x) = 2^{\lfloor \log_2 |x| \rfloor - 10} $$

where $x$ is a world coordinate in metres. At 10 m from the origin that is $2^{-7} \approx 7.8$ mm; at 100 m, 62.5 mm; at 1 km, half a metre; past 65504 m it becomes `inf`. That quantised position feeds the view vector, the distance in the inverse-square falloff, and the shadow-map lookup, so on a large outdoor scene the error is geometric rather than a banding artefact.

Why

The alternative is to store nothing and recover position from depth via `invViewProj * (ndc, depth, 1)`. Both ingredients are present: the `D32_SFLOAT` depth image is still a combined-image-sampler slot in the pass's layout (binding 4), and `invViewProj` occupies the first 64 bytes of the lighting push-constant block. The shader declares nothing at binding 4, and uses `invViewProj` once, for the sky: a pixel with no geometry builds its view ray from the far plane to look up the environment. {{cite shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang "const float4 far = mulColumns(pc.invViewProj, float4(uv * 2.0 - 1.0, 1.0, 1.0));"}} Storing position buys simplicity — no matrix, no NDC bookkeeping, no near/far dependence — for 6 bytes per pixel plus the precision above. Every other reader made the same call: SSAO, SSR, SSS and both RT techniques read the stored position too, and no shader reconstructs position from depth.

What the fragment shader packs

Normals are octahedron-encoded to two channels and then remapped into $[0,1]$.

vec2 encodedNormal = encodeNormalOctahedron(N) * 0.5 + 0.5;shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:221

That remap is a UNORM convention: the image is half-float and could hold negatives, and the remap stays correct only because every reader undoes it symmetrically. Each reader carries its own decoder: the lighting pass its `decodeNormalOctahedron`, and SSAO, SSR and GI's denoiser a `decodeOctahedron` with the same remap.

const float2 f = e * 2.0 - 1.0;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:77

There is no shared encoding header: the encoder lives in `gbuffer_body.glsl` and each reader's decoder in its own file. The pair that must stay in lockstep is the G-buffer's encoder against each reader's decoder.

Where the material comes from is part of the same story. The draws are indirect, and a command's `firstInstance` is the draw's index in `RenderScene`'s draw table, so `gbuffer.vert` begins by reading the draw record at `gl_InstanceIndex` (set 1, binding 5): its instance slot selects the instance, and its material slot goes to the fragment shader as a flat varying.

const uint slot = d.draw.w;shaders/core/gbuffer.vert:39

The fragment shader takes that submesh material slot, resolves it through the instance's material-reference list into a row of `RenderScene`'s material table, and loads six of its seven `vec4` rows (row 3, alpha, only under `OHAO_GBUFFER_MASK`): base colour and albedo texture; roughness, metallic, normal and emissive textures; the rough/metal texture, emission luminance, subsurface and occlusion texture; and OpenPBR's specular, base and emission rows.

vec4 row5 = matRows[m * kMaterialRows + 5u]; // specular_ior, base_diffuse_roughness, base_weight, specularTexshaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:108

The same table feeds the path tracer, so the two renderers cannot disagree about which material a triangle has. A slot the instance has no reference for falls back to material 0.

Bindless texture indices reach the shader by bit-copy rather than conversion; the table packer reinterprets each uint32 as a float:

const auto bits = [](uint32_t u) { return std::bit_cast<float>(u); };ohao/render/scene/material_table.cpp:37

so `UINT32_MAX` arrives as a quiet NaN and is recovered with `floatBitsToUint`. Nothing does arithmetic on the lane, so the pattern survives intact — but the "no texture" tests are not all the same test. Albedo and normal compare against the sentinel; rough/metal, occlusion, emissive and the two specular textures compare against the array capacity.

if (roughMetalTexIdx < 4096u) {shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:195

4096 is the descriptor count the renderer asks `BindlessTextureManager` for at init, so the second form is strictly safer: it also rejects a stale but in-range-looking index that would otherwise read past the end of the array.

if (!m_textureManager->initialize(m_device, m_physicalDevice, nullptr, 4096,ohao/gpu/vulkan/renderer.cpp:127

The rough/metal sample is read for G (roughness) and B (metallic) only, as glTF defines them; occlusion comes from glTF's own occlusion texture. Roughness is stored as authored, with no floor: OpenPBR's module holds it at `kMinSpecularRoughness` (0.01) when it shades, in the lighting pass and the path tracer alike.

rough = clamp(m.specularRoughness, kMinSpecularRoughness, 1.0);shaders/material/openpbr.slang:422

OpenPBR's inputs: two more targets

OpenPBR's base needs more than albedo, roughness and metallic, so the G-buffer has two targets for the rest. `kSpecular` (output 5, `RGBA8_UNORM`) holds `specular_color` and `specular_weight / 1.5`, with the KHR specular textures applied; `kOpenPBR` (output 6, `RG8_UNORM`) holds `base_diffuse_roughness` and `(specular_ior - 1) / 2.55`. The scales are chosen so the defaults are exact codes: a weight of 1 is 170, an IOR of 1.5 is 50.

outSpecular = vec4(clamp(specularColor, 0.0, 1.0), clamp(specularWeight / 1.5, 0.0, 1.0));shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:236
outOpenPBR = vec2(clamp(row5.y, 0.0, 1.0), clamp((row5.x - 1.0) / 2.55, 0.0, 1.0));shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:237

The albedo target carries `base_weight × base_color`, which is OpenPBR's diffuse albedo and a metal's F0 at once; the lighting pass therefore passes a base weight of 1. The lighting pass undoes both encodings and rebuilds `MaterialInputs`:

mi.specularIor = 1.0 + pbr.g * 2.55;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:216

The emissive attachment

Emission is an `RGBA16F` attachment of its own (`kEmissive`, output 4): `emission_color × emission_luminance`, times the emissive texture's colour when there is one (sRGB-decoded by the bindless sampler), the same radiance the path tracer's closest hit resolves:

vec3 emissive = row6.rgb * row2.y;shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:212
float3 emission = row6.rgb * row2.y;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:955

Lighting adds it unlit, in its own colour. The normal target's alpha keeps only its luminance, to keep an emissive surface out of the SSS blur. Because the colour is a material factor (row 6) and the texture only multiplies it, a material with an emission colour and no emissive map still emits.

Velocity: right about Vulkan, and about objects

The motion vector is the raw NDC difference, halved, with no vertical flip.

outVelocity = (currentNDC - prevNDC) * 0.5;shaders/includes/scene/gbuffer_body.glsl:119

Vulkan's NDC y points down, the same direction as texture v, so $uv = 0.5\,ndc + 0.5$ in both axes and the halved difference *is* the UV delta `taa_resolve.frag` subtracts to find history. A GL-derived version needs a `y` negate here; this one must not.

The "previous" position pairs last frame's view-projection with the instance's **previous** world matrix, read from the instance table in the vertex shader:

fragPrevPos = cam.prevViewProj * (inst.prevWorld * vec4(prevLocal, 1.0));shaders/core/gbuffer.vert:62

(`prevLocal` is the vertex's position, or on a skinned draw last frame's deformed one, which the skin pass leaves in `boneWeights.xyz`)

so a moving object is reprojected from where it was, and TAA's history follows it rather than smearing it. What it does not capture is the other half of the pair: the current projection is Halton-jittered for TAA while the stored previous one is not:

m_prevViewProj = m_proj * m_view; // unjittered: next frame's velocity carries its own jitterohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:696

so a static camera over a static scene still writes a non-zero velocity — that frame's jitter offset, and nothing else. It is smaller than it sounds: the Halton offsets are bounded by half a pixel, divided by the render resolution, and halved again on the way from NDC to UV.

return m_jitterSequence[sampleIndex] / glm::vec2(m_width, m_height);ohao/render/deferred/taa_pass.cpp:183

At 1080p that caps the velocity magnitude near $2.7 \times 10^{-4}$; with `motionScale` at 100 it is a `motionWeight` of at most 0.03, moving the history blend from 0.9 to about 0.89. Off the floor, and not by much.

float motionWeight = clamp(velocityMag * params.motionScale, 0.0, 1.0);shaders/postprocess/taa_resolve.frag:148

The lighting pass edits the material it just unpacked

Before it builds the surface, `deferred_lighting.slang` rewrites albedo, roughness and metallic in place through a weather stack — wetness, snow, mud, frost — each gated on its own push-constant scalar. Three of the four are keyed off `N.y`: wetness and mud through `saturate(N.y * 2)`, snow through a steeper `saturate((N.y - 0.3) / 0.7)`, so water pools and snow settles only on upward-facing surfaces.

roughness = lerp(roughness, max(roughness * 0.15, 0.02), w);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:185

Frost is the exception, and it reads as an oversight rather than a choice: its block computes no slope factor at all, so a nonzero `frostCover` lays its smooth pale-blue coating over every surface in the frame with no attenuation whatever — ceilings, undersides and vertical walls included.

if (pc.frostCover > 0.001)shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:200

This is a real authoring feature of the raster path with no counterpart in the RT shaders: the path tracer reads its material from the SSBO and never sees wetness or snow. Only after the stack runs does `makeSurface` build the surface's frame and its `OpenPBRBase` from the edited inputs.

const Surface s = makeSurface(P, N, V, mi);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:218

The froxel's lights, and a falloff engineered not to pop

The pixel's light list comes from `lightList` in `clustered_lights.slang`: the indices the `lights.cluster` pass wrote for the froxel the pixel's screen tile and view depth fall in (the clustered-lighting unit), so a pixel shades the lights that can reach it out of a table of up to 65,536. `directLight` then walks the list and shades each light through OpenPBR's base:

const float3 Lo = directLight(s, lights, clusters, list, clustered, vis, tables);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:231

Point and spot lights use a windowed inverse-square:

$$ \mathrm{atten}(d) = \frac{\big[\mathrm{clamp}\!\left(1 - (d/r)^4,\ 0,\ 1\right)\big]^{2}}{\max(d^{2},\ 0.01)} $$

with $d$ the distance from the shaded point to the light and $r$ the light's range, packed in `direction.w`. The denominator is the physical term, floored so the gain saturates at $100\times$ rather than diverging as $d \to 0$. The numerator is the windowing factor, and it is *squared* on purpose: both the window and its first derivative vanish at $d = r$, making the whole attenuation $C^1$ there, so a light does not visibly snap off at its range boundary. Past $d = r$ the clamp makes it exactly 0, and the loop skips such a light; that hard zero is why the froxel lists, which leave out lights out of range, change no pixel.

return invSq * windowing * windowing;shaders/includes/lighting/clustered_lights.slang:146

What is bound, and what is actually read

Shadowing resolves to one scalar per light iteration, carried in a `LightVisibility`, and RT visibility, when present, wins outright over cascaded shadow maps: the pass computes the CSM term only when RT shadows are off. The RT visibility is an `R32G32_UINT` image of eight 8-bit channels, one per shadow-casting light (x: lights 0-3, y: 4-7), fetched once at the pixel before the loop; each light then picks its own byte by the slot the light table wrote into its `params.w`:

vis.rtWords = vis.rt ? rtShadowVisibility.Load(int3(int2(fragCoord.xy), 0)).xy : uint2(0u);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:229
const uint word = slot < 4 ? vis.rtWords.x : vis.rtWords.y;shaders/includes/lighting/clustered_lights.slang:235

A slot of -1 (the light casts no shadows or is a caster past the first eight) is never occluded; slots 0 to 7 go to the first eight casters in light order.

CSM picks a cascade by positive view-space depth and takes a 3×3 PCF of hardware depth comparisons (a `Sampler2DArrayShadow`); it neither blends between cascades nor applies a normal-offset bias, though the cascade UBO carries `cascadeBlendWidth` and `normalBias` for exactly that. It also shadows only directional lights with a non-negative slot: point and spot lights cast no shadow without RT.

vis.directional = !vis.rt && (pc.flags & 4u) != 0u ? shadowCSM(P, viewDepth) : 1.0;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:230

Ambient is defensive because every input can legitimately be absent. IBL runs under flag bit 0, which the pass raises only when both of `EnvPrefilter`'s images are bound: the environment GGX-prefiltered into `kEnvPrefilterLevels` mips (binding 15) and its irradiance (binding 7). Without an environment both bindings hold the dummy view and the branch never runs.

if (m_prefilteredView != VK_NULL_HANDLE && m_irradianceView != VK_NULL_HANDLE)ohao/render/deferred/deferred_lighting_pass.cpp:182

Inside the branch the split sum reads the prefiltered level for the pixel's roughness along the reflection, weighted by RT GI's environment visibility with GI's bounce radiance for the occluded rest, times the reflection weight. That weight is the specular lobes' own directional albedo toward the viewer, from OpenPBR's energy tables (binding 20) rather than a fitted environment BRDF, times a specular-occlusion term built from the material's `ao` and SSAO (the deferred lighting unit, "Image-based lighting"). It is computed before the branch, for every pixel, because SSR's resolve weights its reflections by it too; the pass writes it and the environment's specular beside the lit image for that resolve (the SSR and SSS unit):

const float3 specularWeight = specularAlbedo * specularOcclusion(NdotV, occlusion, roughness);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:256

Every indirect diffuse term is weighted by the diffuse's directional albedo beneath the specular (`diffuseAlbedo`: EON's, scaled by what the specular lets through, zero for a metal). The constant ambient is the last resort, applied only when the frame has neither RT GI nor an environment; it has no counterpart in the path tracer.

else if (!ibl) ambient += pc.ambientIntensity * diffuseAlbedo * ao * ssao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:249

The prefiltered images are the deferred frame's own; the env image the path tracer and RT GI sample keeps its single mip level and its base-level sampler, so nothing here can move a path-traced pixel:

imgInfo.mipLevels = 1;ohao/gpu/vulkan/env_upload.cpp:85

Screen-space AO does reach these terms. When the effect is on, the render graph runs `executeSSAO`, hands the half-resolution view to the lighting pass, and announces it via flag bit 1:

if (m_ssaoView != VK_NULL_HANDLE) m_params.flags |= 2; // SSAOohao/render/deferred/deferred_lighting_pass.cpp:184

and the shader samples binding 10 under that bit, upsampling the (occlusion, distance) pairs against its own distance from the camera — the four nearest samples weighted bilinearly and by a 5 % distance match, the closest one where none matches:

float ssaoAt(int2 pixel, float dist)shaders/core/deferred_lighting.slang:88

The result multiplies the constant ambient and the IBL terms, on top of the material occlusion channel in GBuffer2's alpha; direct light and RT GI never see it. The deferred SSAO unit covers the pass that produces it.

Key idea

This GBuffer is a lossy re-encoding of the material the path tracer receives, not the material itself: position quantised to half-float, OpenPBR's specular and base inputs quantised to 8 bits, then albedo/roughness/metallic edited again for weather before the BSDF runs. Without weather the BSDF is the same `OpenPBRBase` the path tracer samples; what differs is how light reaches it.

Contracts

  • Background is detected by exact float equality — all four channels of `gBuffer0` zero, matching the clear value. Geometry landing on the world origin with metallic exactly 0 is drawn as sky.
  • `gbuffer_body.glsl`'s `encodeNormalOctahedron` and each reader's decoder are the matched pair, as are the `* 0.5 + 0.5` on write and the `* 2.0 - 1.0` on read. Change either side without the other and every normal in the frame rotates.
  • The `kSpecular` and `kOpenPBR` encodings (`/ 1.5`, `(ior - 1) / 2.55`) are written in `gbuffer_body.glsl` and undone in `deferred_lighting.slang`; change one without the other and every dielectric's reflectance moves.
  • `LightingParams` (C++) and the Slang push-constant block must stay byte-identical at 184 bytes, field for field.
  • Double-sided materials rasterise with `VK_CULL_MODE_NONE` (one-sided ones cull back faces) and the shader has no `gl_FrontFacing` flip, so the visible back faces of a double-sided material store outward-pointing normals and shade dark. `gbuffer.vert` also transforms the tangent by the inverse-transpose normal matrix, correct only under uniform scale.

The decision that shapes the rest: position lives in the buffer

`gbuffer.frag` writes four colour attachments plus depth, and the first one settles the character of the whole pipeline: it stores the interpolated **world position**, verbatim, with metallic in alpha.

outGBuffer0 = vec4(fragWorldPos, metallic);shaders/core/gbuffer.frag:172

The lighting pass reads it straight back and never touches the depth buffer.

vec3 fragPos = gBuffer0Sample.rgb;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:205

That target is `R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT` — IEEE binary16, an 11-bit significand. In the binade $[2^e, 2^{e+1})$ the spacing between representable values is

$$ \mathrm{ulp}(x) = 2^{\lfloor \log_2 |x| \rfloor - 10} $$

where $x$ is a world coordinate in metres. At 10 m from the origin that is $2^{-7} \approx 7.8$ mm; at 100 m, 62.5 mm; at 1 km, half a metre; past 65504 m it becomes `inf`. That quantised position feeds the view vector, the distance in the inverse-square falloff, and the shadow-map lookup, so on a large outdoor scene the error is geometric rather than a banding artefact.

Why

The alternative is to store nothing and recover position from depth via `invViewProj * vec4(ndc, depth, 1)`. Both ingredients are present and correct: the `D32_SFLOAT` depth image goes into descriptor binding 4 every frame, and `invViewProj` occupies the first 64 bytes of the lighting push-constant block. Neither is read — the shader declares no sampler at binding 4, and `invViewProj` appears exactly once in the file, in its own declaration. {{cite shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag " mat4 invViewProj;"}} Storing position buys simplicity — no matrix, no NDC bookkeeping, no near/far dependence — for 6 bytes per pixel plus the precision above. Every other reader made the same call: SSAO, SSR, SSS and both RT techniques read the stored position too, and `reconstruction.glsl`, the depth-to-position helper, now has no includer at all.

What the fragment shader packs

Normals are octahedron-encoded to two channels and then remapped into $[0,1]$.

vec2 encodedNormal = encodeNormalOctahedron(N) * 0.5 + 0.5;shaders/core/gbuffer.frag:184

That remap is a UNORM convention, left from when the target was `R10G10B10A2_UNORM` and could not hold negatives. The allocator's comment still says `RGB10A2` and the shared header still calls itself the `A2R10G10B10` encoder, but the image is half-float now; the remap survives only because `decodeNormalOctahedron` undoes it symmetrically on the read side. That decoder lives in `includes/common/encoding.glsl`, and `deferred_lighting.frag` is now its only consumer; the Slang passes — SSAO, SSR, RT shadows and RT GI — each carry their own `decodeOctahedron` with the same remap.

vec2 f = encoded * 2.0 - 1.0;shaders/includes/common/encoding.glsl:16

The header also carries an `encodeNormalOctahedron` with the same math as the one `gbuffer.frag` defines locally, and it has zero callers anywhere in `shaders/`: the only encode in the tree is the local one. Nothing structural forces that duplication — `gbuffer.frag` enables `GL_GOOGLE_include_directive` and includes `scene_data.glsl` and the generated `shared/layout.glsl`, just not `encoding.glsl`. The pair that must stay in lockstep is `gbuffer.frag`'s encoder against the header's *decoder*; the header's encoder is dead weight.

Where the material comes from is part of the same story. The draws are indirect, and a command's `firstInstance` is the draw's index in `RenderScene`'s draw table, so `gbuffer.vert` begins by reading the draw record at `gl_InstanceIndex` (set 1, binding 5): its instance slot selects the instance, and its material slot goes to the fragment shader as a flat varying.

const uint slot = d.draw.w;shaders/core/gbuffer.vert:39

The fragment shader takes that submesh material slot, resolves it through the instance's material-reference list into a row of `RenderScene`'s material table, and loads three `vec4`s from it: base colour and albedo texture, roughness, metallic, normal and emissive textures, and the rough/metal texture with the emissive and subsurface strengths. The same table feeds the path tracer, so the two renderers cannot disagree about which material a triangle has. A slot the instance has no reference for falls back to material 0.

Bindless texture indices reach the shader by bit-copy rather than conversion; the table packer reinterprets each uint32 as a float:

const auto bits = [](uint32_t u) { return std::bit_cast<float>(u); };ohao/render/scene/material_table.cpp:35

so `UINT32_MAX` arrives as a quiet NaN and is recovered with `floatBitsToUint`. Nothing does arithmetic on the lane, so the pattern survives intact — but the two "no texture" tests are not the same test. Albedo and normal compare against the sentinel; rough/metal and emissive compare against the array capacity.

if (roughMetalTexIdx < 4096u) {shaders/core/gbuffer.frag:158

4096 is the descriptor count the renderer asks `BindlessTextureManager` for at init — the value that becomes the variable-length array's `descriptorCount` — so the second form is strictly safer: it also rejects a stale but in-range-looking index that would otherwise read past the end of the array.

if (!m_textureManager->initialize(m_device, m_physicalDevice, nullptr, 4096,ohao/gpu/vulkan/renderer.cpp:127

That sample is then read as ORM — R occlusion, G roughness, B metallic — though glTF defines only G and B, so an asset that packs nothing into R darkens by whatever sits there.

The emissive attachment

Emission is the G-buffer's fifth colour output, an `RGBA16F` attachment of its own: the emissive texture's colour (sRGB-decoded by the bindless sampler) times the material's emissive strength, the same radiance the path tracer's closest-hit shader emits:

emissive = texture(textures[nonuniformEXT(emissiveTexIdx)], fragTexCoord).rgb * row2.y;shaders/core/gbuffer.frag:178
emissive = textureLod(textures[nonuniformEXT(emissiveTexIdx)], texUV, 0.0).rgb * matParams2.y;shaders/rt/pt_closesthit.rchit:180

Lighting adds it unlit, in its own colour. It used to arrive as one luminance scalar in the normal target's alpha, re-tinted with the albedo, so an emitter glowed in its base colour: DamagedHelmet's cyan ring rendered dim grey. The alpha still carries that luminance, only to keep an emissive surface out of the SSS blur. Emission cannot glow *hard* yet: the strength lane, `row2.y`, is set to exactly `1.0f` for any material that has an emissive texture and left at 0 otherwise — model upload has no other path that raises it — and the bindless textures it multiplies are 8-bit, so the radiance is bounded by 1.0.

if (m.emissiveTex != kNoTexture) m.emissiveStrength = 1.0f;ohao/render/scene/model_upload.cpp:218

The sharper limitation is upstream of the shader: a material has no emissive colour factor at all, only a texture index and that strength. The shader gates on the *index*, so a material without a resolvable emissive map contributes nothing here regardless of what the source asset's emissive factor said.

Velocity: right about Vulkan, and about objects

The motion vector is the raw NDC difference, halved, with no vertical flip.

outVelocity = (currentNDC - prevNDC) * 0.5;shaders/core/gbuffer.frag:103

Vulkan's NDC y points down, the same direction as texture v, so $uv = 0.5\,ndc + 0.5$ in both axes and the halved difference *is* the UV delta `taa_resolve.frag` subtracts to find history. A GL-derived version needs a `y` negate here; this one must not.

The "previous" position pairs last frame's view-projection with the instance's **previous** world matrix, read from the instance table in the vertex shader:

fragPrevPos = cam.prevViewProj * (inst.prevWorld * vec4(prevLocal, 1.0));shaders/core/gbuffer.vert:62

(`prevLocal` is the vertex's position, or on a skinned draw last frame's deformed one, which the skin pass leaves in `boneWeights.xyz`)

so a moving object is reprojected from where it was, and TAA's history follows it rather than smearing it. What it does not capture is the other half of the pair: the current projection is Halton-jittered for TAA while the stored previous one is not:

m_prevViewProj = m_proj * m_view; // unjittered: next frame's velocity carries its own jitterohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:666

so a static camera over a static scene still writes a non-zero velocity — that frame's jitter offset, and nothing else. It is smaller than it sounds: the Halton offsets are bounded by half a pixel, divided by the render resolution, and halved again on the way from NDC to UV.

return m_jitterSequence[sampleIndex] / glm::vec2(m_width, m_height);ohao/render/deferred/taa_pass.cpp:182

At 1080p that caps the velocity magnitude near $2.7 \times 10^{-4}$; with `motionScale` at 100 it is a `motionWeight` of at most 0.03, moving the history blend from 0.9 to about 0.89. Off the floor, and not by much.

float motionWeight = clamp(velocityMag * params.motionScale, 0.0, 1.0);shaders/postprocess/taa_resolve.frag:145

The lighting pass edits the material it just unpacked

Before any BRDF work, `deferred_lighting.frag` rewrites albedo, roughness and metallic in place through a weather stack — wetness, snow, mud, frost — each gated on its own push-constant scalar. Three of the four are keyed off `N.y`: wetness and mud through `clamp(N.y * 2, 0, 1)`, snow through a steeper `clamp((N.y - 0.3) / 0.7, 0, 1)`, so water pools and snow settles only on upward-facing surfaces.

roughness = mix(roughness, max(roughness * 0.15, 0.02), w);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:220

Frost is the exception, and it reads as an oversight rather than a choice: its block computes no slope factor at all, so a nonzero `frostCover` lays its smooth pale-blue coating over every surface in the frame with no attenuation whatever — ceilings, undersides and vertical walls included.

if (pc.frostCover > 0.001) {shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:244

This is a real authoring feature of the raster path with no counterpart in the RT shaders: the path tracer reads its material from the SSBO and never sees wetness or snow. Only after the stack runs is `initBRDFSurface` called, where the metallic slider gets its `smoothstep(0.35, 0.65)` sharpening.

The froxel's lights, and a falloff engineered not to pop

The loop runs over the pixel's froxel's light list, the indices the `lights.cluster` pass wrote for the froxel the pixel's screen tile and view depth fall in (the clustered-lighting unit), so a pixel shades the lights that can reach it out of a table of up to 65,536:

lightCount = clusterData[cluster];shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:276

Point and spot lights use a windowed inverse-square:

$$ \mathrm{atten}(d) = \frac{\big[\mathrm{clamp}\!\left(1 - (d/r)^4,\ 0,\ 1\right)\big]^{2}}{\max(d^{2},\ 0.01)} $$

with $d$ the distance from the shaded point to the light and $r$ the light's range, packed in `direction.w`. The denominator is the physical term, floored so the gain saturates at $100\times$ rather than diverging as $d \to 0$. The numerator is the windowing factor, and it is *squared* on purpose: both the window and its first derivative vanish at $d = r$, making the whole attenuation $C^1$ there, so a light does not visibly snap off at its range boundary. Past $d = r$ the clamp makes it exactly 0, and the loop skips such a light; that hard zero is why the froxel lists, which leave out lights out of range, change no pixel.

return invSq * windowing * windowing;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:96

What is bound, and what is actually read

Shadowing resolves to one scalar per light iteration, and RT visibility, when present, wins outright over cascaded shadow maps — the CSM branch is `else if`, not an extra multiply. The RT visibility is an `R32G32_UINT` image of eight 8-bit channels, one per shadow-casting light (x: lights 0-3, y: 4-7), bound as a `usampler2D` and fetched once at `gl_FragCoord` before the loop; each light then picks its own byte by the slot the light table wrote into its `params.w`:

const uvec2 rtShadow = rtShadows ? texelFetch(rtShadowVisibility, ivec2(gl_FragCoord.xy), 0).xy : uvec2(0u);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:280
const uint word = slot < 4 ? rtShadow.x : rtShadow.y;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:316

A slot of -1 (the light casts no shadows, is an emissive proxy, or is a caster past the first eight) is never occluded; slots 0 to 7 go to the first eight casters in light order.

CSM picks a cascade by positive view-space depth and takes a 3×3 PCF of hardware depth comparisons (a `sampler2DArrayShadow`); it neither blends between cascades nor applies a normal-offset bias, though the cascade UBO carries `cascadeBlendWidth` and `normalBias` for exactly that. It also runs only for `lightType == 0` with a non-negative slot: point and spot lights cast no shadow without RT.

Ambient is defensive because every input can legitimately be absent. IBL runs under flag bit 0, which the pass raises only when both of `EnvPrefilter`'s images are bound: the environment GGX-prefiltered into `kEnvPrefilterLevels` mips (binding 15) and its irradiance (binding 7). Without an environment both bindings hold the dummy view and the branch never runs.

if (m_prefilteredView != VK_NULL_HANDLE && m_irradianceView != VK_NULL_HANDLE)ohao/render/deferred/deferred_lighting_pass.cpp:176

Inside the branch the split sum reads the prefiltered level for the pixel's roughness along the reflection, weighted by RT GI's environment visibility with GI's bounce radiance for the occluded rest, times the reflection weight: Karis's analytic environment BRDF with the material's `ao` and SSAO applied through a specular-occlusion term (the deferred lighting unit, "Image-based lighting"). The weight is computed before the branch, for every pixel, because SSR's resolve weights its reflections by it too; the pass writes it and the environment's specular beside the lit image for that resolve (the SSR and SSS unit):

const vec3 specularWeight = (surface.F0 * ab.x + ab.y) * specularOcclusion(NdotV, occlusion, roughness);shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:360

The constant ambient term ahead of it is the last resort, applied only when the frame has neither RT GI nor an environment, and diffuse only, `albedo * (1 - metallic)`: on a metal it used to add a flat F0-tinted glow, and on every wall a flat floor of light under RT GI's bounce, neither of which the path tracer has.

The prefiltered images are the deferred frame's own; the env image the path tracer and RT GI sample keeps its single mip level and its base-level sampler, so nothing here can move a path-traced pixel:

imgInfo.mipLevels = 1;ohao/gpu/vulkan/env_upload.cpp:64

Screen-space AO does reach these terms. When the effect is on, the render graph runs `executeSSAO`, hands the half-resolution view to the lighting pass, and announces it via flag bit 1:

if (m_ssaoView != VK_NULL_HANDLE) m_params.flags |= 2; // SSAOohao/render/deferred/deferred_lighting_pass.cpp:178

and the shader samples binding 10 under that bit, upsampling the (occlusion, distance) pairs against its own distance from the camera — the four nearest samples weighted bilinearly and by a 5 % distance match, the closest one where none matches:

float ssaoAt(ivec2 pixel, float dist) {shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:108

The result multiplies the constant ambient and the IBL terms, on top of the material occlusion channel in GBuffer2's alpha; direct light and RT GI never see it. The deferred SSAO unit covers the pass that produces it.

Key idea

This GBuffer is not a compressed encoding of a surface but a lossy *re-authoring* of one: position quantised to half-float, emission reduced to a luminance the lighting pass re-tints with albedo, then albedo/roughness/metallic edited again for weather before the BRDF runs. The deferred image is not showing the material the path tracer receives.

Contracts

  • Background is detected by exact float equality — `gBuffer0.rgb == vec3(0)` **and** `.a == 0`, matching the clear value. Geometry landing on the world origin with metallic exactly 0 is discarded as sky.
  • `gbuffer.frag`'s local `encodeNormalOctahedron` and `encoding.glsl`'s `decodeNormalOctahedron` are the matched pair, as are the `* 0.5 + 0.5` on write and the `* 2.0 - 1.0` on read. Change either side without the other and every normal in the frame rotates. The header's own `encodeNormalOctahedron` is not part of that contract — it has no callers, and editing it changes nothing.
  • `LightingParams` (C++) and the GLSL push-constant block must stay byte-identical at 184 bytes, including the unread `invViewProj` and `screenSize` fields.
  • Double-sided materials rasterise with `VK_CULL_MODE_NONE` (one-sided ones cull back faces) and the shader has no `gl_FrontFacing` flip, so the visible back faces of a double-sided material store outward-pointing normals and shade dark. `gbuffer.vert` also transforms the tangent by the inverse-transpose normal matrix, correct only under uniform scale.

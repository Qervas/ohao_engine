Three encodings REBLUR insists on

NVIDIA's REBLUR is a spatio-temporal accumulator that reads meaning out of the bits you hand it, and it refuses almost everything a path tracer naturally produces. Its diffuse and specular inputs must be *demodulated* (surface colour divided out), carried in *YCoCg* rather than linear RGB, and their alpha channel must hold a hit distance already *normalized* into [0,1] by NRD's own view-depth-and-roughness formula. Get either of the first two wrong and the failure is not subtle noise — it is a hue shift; get the third wrong and REBLUR quietly filters at the wrong radius.

One producer writes those inputs: `path_tracer.slang`, which both RT profiles run. It is Slang and packs inline. The consumer side is GLSL: `nrd_compose.comp` includes `nrd_frontend.glsl`, which holds both directions of the YCoCg rotation and the forward hit-distance map, though compose calls only the inverse rotation. Neither half of the demodulation lives in a shared file: the divide is in the raygen and the matching multiply is in `nrd_compose.comp`.

NRD is the realtime profile's denoiser wherever DLSS Ray Reconstruction cannot run (a build without the SDK, or a GPU not NVIDIA's), and the offline profile runs it when asked:

.denoiseMode = DenoiseMode::NRD, // a moving view is one sample a frame: the denoiser keeps the historyohao/render/rt/rt_settings.hpp:37

Demodulation, and why only the diffuse gets divided

Temporal filters smooth *lighting*, and lighting is smooth; albedo is not. Radiance that still carries texture detail makes the filter fight the texture, so the diffuse channel is divided by first-hit diffuse albedo before packing, with a floor to survive metals, where diffuse albedo is zero:

const float3 diffDemod = diffuseOut / max(diffAlbedo, float3(0.03));shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:700
diffAlbedo = base.rho * (1.0 - base.metalness);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:609

The specular channel is divided by nothing, and compose multiplies it by nothing: its radiance comes from sampling the whole BSDF, Fresnel included, so it already is the reflected light. Binding 25 still carries the specular albedo toward the viewer — the OpenPBR base's directional albedo minus its diffuse part — as a guide for DLSS Ray Reconstruction; compose binds it and does not read it.

specColor = max(a - diffAlbedo, float3(0.0));shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:610

What the camera ray itself meets and that emits — a light's emitter mesh, an emissive surface, the sky — is in neither channel. It has no albedo to divide out and nothing to filter, so the raygen writes it to its own guide at binding 13, and compose adds it after REBLUR:

emissionAOV[pixel] = lerp(emissionAOV[pixel], float4(add, 1.0), blendAOVs ? 1.0 / float(pc.control.y + 1u) : 1.0);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:527

How the lobes are split

At the primary hit the split is by lobe evaluation. The light sample and the environment sample each evaluate the BSDF as its two parts, specular and diffuse, and each part goes to its own channel:

if (depth == 0u) { diffuseOut += throughput * fd * ls.radiance * w; specularOut += throughput * fs * ls.radiance * w; }shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:636

Every later vertex goes to the channel of the lobe the first BSDF sample drew:

if (firstLobe == 1u) diffuseOut += add;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:521

So a direct highlight travels in the specular channel, filtered by REBLUR's specular path at the surface's roughness, and indirect light follows the lobe that carried the path off the surface.

Why

One path a sample keeps the beauty a single unbiased estimate: the channels partition what the path adds, they do not trace twice. Past the first vertex the partition is coarse — a channel is chosen once per path, by its first bounce — but direct light, most of most images, is split exactly.

YCoCg, and the two ways to get magenta

The forward transform is the standard reversible luma/chroma rotation ported from `NRD.hlsli`, where $Y$ is luma and $C_o,C_g$ are the orange and green chroma axes:

$$ Y=\tfrac14 R+\tfrac12 G+\tfrac14 B,\qquad C_o=\tfrac12(R-B),\qquad C_g=-\tfrac14 R+\tfrac12 G-\tfrac14 B $$

The reference tracer writes the same three dot products inline:

return float4(dot(c, float3(0.25, 0.5, 0.25)), dot(c, float3(0.5, 0.0, -0.5)), dot(c, float3(-0.25, 0.5, -0.25)), normHd);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:436

The inverse is done without a matrix: $t=Y-C_g$ recovers $(R+B)/2$, so $G=Y+C_g$, $R=t+C_o$, $B=t-C_o$. Algebraically exact — but the unpack still clamps, because a spatio-temporal filter reconstructs a value that was never in the original gamut and can land slightly negative:

return max(r, vec3(0.0));shaders/includes/rt/nrd_frontend.glsl:24

Two distinct mistakes produce the same magenta symptom. Skip the pack, and REBLUR reads red as luma and green/blue as chroma; compose then rotates an already-linear triple through the inverse basis, which its unpack names:

// Back-end unpack: REBLUR writes YCoCg in .rgb (see REBLUR_BackEnd_UnpackRadianceAndNormHitDist).shaders/rt/nrd_compose.comp:30

Pack correctly but skip demodulation, and the input is in the right basis but the wrong space — the filter's cross-channel behaviour drifts on albedo-coloured radiance, and compose applies the diffuse albedo a second time: a hue shift plus a visible darkening.

Key idea

Magenta in the NRD path is almost never a denoiser bug. It means a producer and a consumer disagree about which colour space binding 22/23 is in. Check the pack before you touch anything in NRD.

The hit distance is a control signal, not data

REBLUR uses alpha to decide how hard to filter each pixel, so the world-space ray length has to be mapped into [0,1] against a scene-scale reference. With $d$ the ray length, $z$ the first hit's view-space depth and $s\in[0,1]$ a lobe-spread term:

$$ f=(A+|z|B)\cdot\mathrm{lerp}(C,1,s),\qquad \hat d=\mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\tfrac{d}{f},0,1\right) $$

Nothing reads $z$ back from binding 20. The raygen computes it at the primary hit, keeps it in a local, and writes it to that binding for NRD's separate `IN_VIEWZ` slot; the normalization uses the local:

viewZ = dot(hit.position - camPos, fwd);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:537

The constants are NRD's `ReblurHitDistanceParameters` defaults, as literals in the raygen's pack and again in the GLSL header:

const float f = (3.0 + abs(viewZ) * 0.1) * lerp(20.0, 1.0, smc);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:433
const vec3 NRD_HIT_DIST_PARAMS = vec3(3.0, 0.1, 20.0);shaders/includes/rt/nrd_frontend.glsl:9

$s$ is where OHAO knowingly diverges. NRD derives it from a richer function of roughness; the raygen substitutes $\text{roughness}^2$:

const float smc = saturate(roughness * roughness);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:432

$d$ is one segment: the length of the first bounce ray, given to the channel of the lobe that drew it, and 0 to the other. A mirror therefore reports the distance to the mirror's first reflected hit, not the length of a chain of reflections.

if (depth == 1u) secondHitT = (hit.flags & kHit) != 0u ? hit.t : 0.0;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:517

The sharp edge is that $A$ exists three times. The raygen hardcodes it at 3.0, the GLSL header at 3.0, and the C++ profile re-declares it and pushes it into `ReblurSettings` — while $B$ and $C$ are left at NRD's defaults and have no C++ knob at all:

s.hitDistanceParameters.A = p.hitDistanceParamA;ohao/render/rt/denoise/nrd_denoise.cpp:232

Change `hitDistanceParamA` and the halves normalize against different scales, with no build-time check that they agree.

When the chain runs

REBLUR and compose run only in NRD mode, on top of the denoiser existing and auxiliary AOVs being on:

if (m_nrdDenoiser && m_renderSettings.enableAuxiliaryAOVs && m_renderSettings.denoiseMode == DenoiseMode::NRD) {ohao/render/rt/path_tracer_render.cpp:699

Both write their descriptor sets at record time, so each keeps one set per frame in flight, selected by the frame slot the tracer passes in.

Lending NRD a device it did not create

NRD's Vulkan path goes through NRI, which normally creates its own device. Here it is handed OHAO's, described by a `DeviceCreationVKDesc` carrying the extension lists the app passed to `vkCreateInstance` and `vkCreateDevice`. The instance list is copied verbatim:

m_impl->instanceExtensionsStorage.assign(instanceExtensions.begin(), instanceExtensions.end());ohao/render/rt/denoise/nrd_denoise.cpp:89

The device list loses three. NRI's dispatch-table resolver eagerly looks up `vkCmdTraceRaysIndirect2KHR` whenever it sees `VK_KHR_ray_tracing_pipeline`, and that entry point lives in `VK_KHR_ray_tracing_maintenance1`, which OHAO does not enable. REBLUR is compute-only, so acceleration-structure, RT-pipeline and deferred-host-operations names are filtered out of the NRI-facing copy:

std::strcmp(e, VK_KHR_RAY_TRACING_PIPELINE_EXTENSION_NAME) == 0 ||ohao/render/rt/denoise/nrd_denoise.cpp:86

Resources cross as raw `VkImage` plus `VkFormat`, not image views — NRI builds its own views from the format. All seven slots are declared as already being in `SHADER_RESOURCE_STORAGE` (`VK_IMAGE_LAYOUT_GENERAL`). For the five inputs that is where the raygen leaves them. The two `OUT_` images are written by no shader before this point; the host puts them in `GENERAL` with an explicit `UNDEFINED`→`GENERAL` barrier recorded ahead of the trace, in the same block that transitions the AOVs:

aovBarriers[10].image = m_outDiffRadianceImage;ohao/render/rt/path_tracer_render.cpp:473

The snapshot then asks for the layouts to be restored afterwards so compose and the host readbacks find what they expect:

snapshot.restoreInitialState = true;ohao/render/rt/denoise/nrd_denoise.cpp:298

Compose is deliberately trivial

`NrdCompositor` owns a standalone compute pipeline whose descriptor set layout is independent of the path tracer's RT layout: seven storage images, no push constants, an 8×8 workgroup. The shader unpacks, remodulates the diffuse, adds the specular and the camera ray's emission:

vec3 composed = diffRad * diffAlbedo + specRad + emission;shaders/rt/nrd_compose.comp:36

It writes that sum twice: as HDR, and as the frame's output (binding 6, the tracer's RGBA8 image) with the tracer's own curve, ACES at 0.5 and 2.2 gamma. A window therefore presents the denoised frame, and a capture under NRD reads the same image any other mode leaves there:

imageStore(outLdr, p, vec4(pow(aces(composed * 0.5), vec3(1.0 / 2.2)), 1.0));shaders/rt/nrd_compose.comp:39

Alpha is dropped. NRD's own input contract defines the normalized hit distance as ambient occlusion on the diffuse channel and specular occlusion on the specular one, and says the `.w` of REBLUR's output carries the filtered version — a signal an application can spend on second-bounce diffuse or on damping IBL. OHAO spends it on nothing: compose reads `.rgb` and discards `.w`, so here it really is only a control signal for REBLUR's own filter widths.

Tuning for a path tracer instead of a game

REBLUR ships with a 30-frame linear accumulation window. The profile pushes the window to REBLUR's documented ceiling of 63 instead — the code states both numbers where it makes the change:

// Maximum linearly accumulated frames. NRD default 30; we bump to 63ohao/render/rt/denoise/nrd_denoise.hpp:119

What NRD is fed depends on the profile. Realtime NRD takes this frame's sample alone: the trace keeps no running mean (`tracerAccumulates` is false) and REBLUR carries the whole history. Offline NRD denoises the mean of a still view's samples: the five guide images become a running mean too, each blended with its previous value at weight $1/(n{+}1)$, where $n$ is the frames already in the mean:

const bool blendAOVs = accumulateAOVs && pc.control.y > 0u;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:454
return s.denoiseMode == DenoiseMode::NRD && !isRealtimeProfile(s.profile);ohao/render/rt/rt_meta.hpp:47

That blend is an image load of the previous dispatch's contents, and the mean is only as good as those contents. Every `render()` re-records the AOV transition block with `oldLayout = VK_IMAGE_LAYOUT_UNDEFINED`, which the spec explicitly permits an implementation to treat as a discard — the running average is a property the code relies on rather than one Vulkan guarantees:

aovBarriers[5].oldLayout = VK_IMAGE_LAYOUT_UNDEFINED;ohao/render/rt/path_tracer_render.cpp:426

The other half of the temporal story is when history is thrown away. The frame index handed to NRD is the denoisers' clock, which only `resetAccumulation` restarts; a moved view in the realtime profile restarts nothing, and NRD reprojects through the motion vectors and last frame's view and projection, which the host captures every frame:

camera.frameIndex = m_historyFrameCount; // 0 only after resetAccumulation: a moved view reprojectsohao/render/rt/path_tracer_render.cpp:727
m_prevViewMatrix = viewM;ohao/render/rt/path_tracer_render.cpp:735

The offline profile resets accumulation on a view change, so offline NRD starts from frame 0 after every move.

Contracts

  • Bindings 22/23 are YCoCg + normalized hit distance, and 27/28 come back the same way. A consumer that reads them as linear RGB — a debug visualizer, say — shows scrambled hue, not an obvious error.
  • The 3.0 literal in `path_tracer.slang`'s pack, `NRD_HIT_DIST_PARAMS.x` in `nrd_frontend.glsl` and `NrdReblurProfile::hitDistanceParamA` must agree. Changing one desynchronizes the normalization silently; nothing checks.
  • `setCommonSettings()` must run before every `denoise()` — it carries the frame index and the previous view/projection NRD reprojects from. `setInputResources()` only stashes handles into the impl and they persist across frames, so the per-frame call is redundant; what breaks is failing to re-stash after any AOV image is recreated, since `denoise()` forwards whatever is there with no validation.
  • The specular channel already carries Fresnel and is never multiplied by binding 25; binding 25 is a guide for DLSS only. The camera ray's emission (binding 13) bypasses REBLUR and is added in compose: any new emissive term at the primary hit belongs there, not in a channel.
  • Compose writes the frame's output with the tracer's curve; a change to that curve must change the raygen, `nrd_compose.comp` and `dlss_tonemap.comp` together.
  • Offline NRD's AOV mean assumes the previous dispatch's image contents survive an `UNDEFINED`→`GENERAL` transition. Nothing in the API promises that; a driver that discards turns the mean into the last sample.
  • NRD's frame index must stay the denoisers' clock, not the trace's running-mean count: a moved view restarts the mean but must not restart REBLUR's history.
  • REBLUR and compose run only when the denoiser exists, auxiliary AOVs are on and the denoise mode is NRD. The raygen still writes the AOVs whenever they are on.

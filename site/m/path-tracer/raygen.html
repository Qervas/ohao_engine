One raygen, both profiles

Both ray-tracing profiles bind the same ray-generation program: `PathTracerShaderSet`'s default, the path tracer in `rt/path_tracer.slang`, whose four stages are entry points of one Slang file:

const char* raygenSpv{"bin/shaders/rt_path_tracer.slang.raygen.spv"};ohao/render/rt/path_tracer.hpp:61
: RTProfileRendererBase(kRealtimeRTSettings, PathTracerShaderSet{}) {} // rt/path_tracer.slangohao/render/rt/rt_profile_renderer.hpp:178

The base installs the set on the `PathTracer` before the pipeline is built:

m_pathTracer.setShaderSet(m_shaderSet);ohao/render/rt/rt_profile_renderer.hpp:90

The program is a correct estimator of OpenPBR's base, the reference the deferred frame is judged by. The profiles differ only in settings: the realtime one traces two bounces a frame, ends its paths in the radiance cache past the first vertex and hands each frame to NRD; the offline one traces 32 into a running mean (see the profiles page). The same file has a second raygen, `cacheUpdate`, which fills the cache (the radiance cache unit).

// The realtime profile ends a path in the radiance cache (rt/radiance_cache.slang, kFlagCache) atshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:24

One path a sample

`path_tracer.slang`'s raygen traces one path per pixel per dispatch through OpenPBR's base BSDF (`material/openpbr.slang`). At every surface vertex it does three things: next-event estimation to one light, next-event estimation to the environment, and one BSDF sample for the next direction.

// Next-event estimation: a light, then the environment, each weighed against the BSDF.shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:619

The camera ray traces with `kRayMaskCamera` (scene, light emitters, first-person guns); every later ray with the scene and the emitters only:

const PathPayload hit = trace(origin, dir, depth == 0u ? kRayMaskCamera : (kRayMaskScene | kTlasMaskEmitter), 1.0e4);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:486

So a bounce ray can meet a light's emitter mesh. That is how every light is found twice, by light sampling and by BSDF sampling, and why the two strategies are weighted against each other at any roughness. A ray that misses ends the path with the environment's radiance; one that meets an emitter ends it with the light's.

Choosing a light

`sampleLight` picks one light uniformly from the RT light buffer and draws a direction by the light's shape:

  • a **sphere** (point and spot lights) uniformly inside its cone of directions,

with the solid-angle density $1/(2\pi(1-\cos\theta_{\max}))$ times the choice probability $1/N$;

  • a **rectangle** (area lights) by area, converted to solid angle by

$d^2/(\cos\theta_\ell A)$;

  • a **sun** (directional) as a delta: its irradiance divided by the choice

probability, with no density to weigh.

s.pdf = choose / (kTwoPi * oneMinusCosMax);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:292
s.pdf = choose * d2 / (cosL * l.area);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:268
s.radiance = l.radiance / choose; // a delta: no density to weighshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:254

The radiances are the deferred frame's: a point light of intensity $I$ is a sphere of radius $r$ (at least 1 cm) and radiance $I/(\pi r^2)$, windowed by its range, a spot the same times its cone toward the shaded point, a rectangle $I/(4\pi r^2)$. `1 - cos` is computed as $\sin^2/(1+\sqrt{1-\sin^2})$, which keeps a distant small sphere from cancelling to zero.

const float oneMinusCos = sin2 / (1.0 + sqrt(1.0 - sin2)); // 1 - cos without cancellationshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:220

The shadow ray stops at the sphere's near side (or 1 mm short of the rectangle's point), so a light never occludes its own sample.

Weighting the strategies

Light sampling and BSDF sampling estimate the same light, so each contribution is weighted by the power heuristic. For a light sample drawn with density $p_\ell$ in direction $\omega_i$:

$$ C_{\text{NEE}} = T\,\frac{f(\omega_o,\omega_i)\cos\theta_i\,L(\omega_i)}{p_\ell}\,\frac{p_\ell^2}{p_\ell^2 + p_f^2} $$

$T$ is the path throughput, $f\cos\theta_i$ is what `OpenPBRBase::eval` returns, and $p_f$ is the BSDF's density for the same direction (`OpenPBRBase::pdf`, the mixture of its GGX and EON lobes). A bounce ray that meets the light's emitter, or escapes to the environment, takes the mirror weight $p_f^2/(p_f^2 + p_\ell^2)$, with $p_f$ the density the last bounce was drawn with and $p_\ell$ what light or environment sampling would have had for that direction.

add = throughput * le * powerHeuristic(lastPdf, lightPdf);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:509
const float w = depth == 0u ? 1.0 : powerHeuristic(lastPdf, envPdf(dir));shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:493

Two cases skip the weight. A sun is a delta, found only by light sampling. A light without an emitter mesh (`LightTable::setEmitterLights` did not flag it) is also found only by light sampling, so it is taken at full weight:

// A sun is a delta (its radiance already over its choice); a light without anshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:630
const float3 c = throughput * f * ls.radiance * w;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:633

A bounce ray that meets an emitter learns which light it is from the emitter instance's `emitterLight.w`, which the closest hit returns as `payload.light`; `emitterRadiance` then recomputes the light's radiance toward the ray's origin and the light-sampling density it would have had.

Why

The power heuristic, not the balance heuristic: when one strategy's density is much larger than the other's (a mirror against a large light, a small light against a diffuse surface), the power heuristic gives the better one almost all the weight and leaves less variance from the weaker one. Because the emitter meshes are in the bounce ray's mask, no roughness threshold is needed to decide which strategy owns a glossy reflection of a light.

Emissive surfaces are not in the light list (the raster frame's emissive proxies are left out of the RT buffer); a path finds them by BSDF sampling only, at full weight.

add = throughput * hit.emission; // emissive surfaces: found by BSDF sampling onlyshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:515

The environment

When the environment has a CDF, each vertex draws one direction from it (`sampleEnv`: a row by the marginal, a texel by the conditional, a point uniformly in the texel) and weighs it against the BSDF in the same way. `envPdf` evaluates the density of any direction from the same two tables, so the miss of a bounce ray is weighted against exactly the density the sampler used. Its map is row 0 at the zenith, as every environment lookup in the engine is:

const float2 uv = float2(atan2(dir.z, dir.x) / kTwoPi + 0.5, 0.5 - asin(clamp(dir.y, -1.0, 1.0)) / kPi); // row 0 the zenithshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:327

The next direction, and when the path ends

`OpenPBRBase::sample` picks the specular lobe with the probability of its share of the albedo's luminance, samples GGX's bounded visible normals or EON's diffuse lobe, and returns $f\cos\theta/p$ with $p$ the mixture density. That weight multiplies the throughput, and the density is kept for the next vertex's MIS.

BsdfSample sample(float3 wo, float3 u, OpenPBRTables t)shaders/material/openpbr.slang:536

A sample below the geometric surface ends the path (the base has no transmission). From the third vertex on, Russian roulette continues with probability $q = \min(\max(T_r, T_g, T_b), 0.95)$ and divides the throughput by it, which keeps the estimator unbiased:

const float q = min(max(throughput.r, max(throughput.g, throughput.b)), 0.95);shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:676

The bounce cap is `params.w`'s low 16 bits; the offline profile asks for 32, which roulette rarely reaches. A cap of 0 still adds the emitter or environment a direct light sample's companion BSDF ray meets, so 0 is direct light, both strategies weighted.

if (stop || depth > bounces) break;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:591

Ending in the radiance cache

With `kFlagCache` set (bit 5 of `control.x`, the realtime profile), a path can end before its cap. At a vertex past the first, the raygen looks the surface up in the radiance cache, if the segment that reached it is at least the cell's voxel long (0.05 m × 2^level, the level from the distance to the camera) and the lobe that drew it spreads over the voxel: a diffuse lobe, or a specular one with roughness² × length at least the voxel. A sharp reflection keeps tracing, so mirrors stay sharp.

static const uint kFlagCache = 32u; // realtime: paths end in the radiance cacheshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:82
if (hit.t >= voxel && (lastLobe == 1u || lastRough * lastRough * hit.t >= voxel))shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:573

The query point is jittered within the voxel's face (Binder 2019) by a hash of the pixel, the sample and the depth, so no cell shows as a block. A hit adds the throughput times the cell's resolved radiance, to the lobe the first bounce chose, and ends the path; a miss (no cell yet) traces on as if the cache were off.

if (cacheLookup(cacheKeys, cacheResolved, cacheCell(hit.position + jt * j.x + jb * j.y, hit.geoNormal, camPos),shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:580

The cache's three buffers are bindings 39–41 (keys, the frame's sums, resolved radiance):

[[vk::binding(39, 0)]] RWStructuredBuffer<uint> cacheKeys; // the radiance cache (rt/radiance_cache.slang)shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:61

Where the random numbers come from

Every draw is a coordinate of Owen-scrambled, shuffled Sobol (`sampling/sobol.slang`, Burley 2020). A pixel's samples are one sequence, indexed by the sample index, so its frames stay stratified however many accumulate; pixels are decorrelated by a seed hashed from the pixel alone.

Sampler rng = Sampler(pc.params.z, pcg(pixel.x ^ pcg(pixel.y ^ 0x7F4A7C15u)));shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:455

Draws come in groups of four, each group one 4-D Sobol point scrambled by a seed hashed from the pixel's and the group's number, so dimensions in one group are jointly stratified and different groups are independent. The raygen places related draws in one group: group 0 is the pixel jitter; at vertex $d$, group $1+3d$ the light choice and its point, $2+3d$ the environment texel and its point, $3+3d$ the BSDF's three numbers and the Russian roulette test.

// bounce d's light choice and point in group 1 + 3d, its environment sample in 2 + 3d, its BSDFshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:117

A branch that skips a draw cannot shift later decisions onto other dimensions: each group starts at its own dimension. The PCG hash is left for the pixel seed and the any-hit shader's coin for blended surfaces.

Shadow rays carry no boolean

A visibility ray starts its payload as a hit, skips the closest-hit shader and stops at the first hit; only the miss shader clears the flag. "The flag is clear" is the unoccluded test.

p.flags = kHit; // the closest hit is skipped: only the miss shader clears itshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:398

Rays leave 1 mm off the surface along the geometric normal on the side the ray came from, and a light or environment sample on the other side of that normal is rejected.

What the raygen writes

Besides radiance, the raygen writes the running mean of every sample since the view last moved (binding 1, read from the previous frame's image at binding 3), the ACES image at exposure 0.5 (binding 2), OIDN's albedo and normal, and NRD's and DLSS's guides. The host says how many frames the mean already holds in `control.y`; 0 means this frame's sample alone, which is what a temporal denoiser takes.

acc = (prev.rgb * prev.w + radiance) / count;shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:695

Motion vectors are written whenever last frame's view-projection belongs to a rendered frame (`control.z`), whether or not the trace accumulates, so a temporal denoiser can reproject across a moved view:

if (pc.control.z != 0u)shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:538

What the camera ray itself meets emits (a light's emitter mesh, an emissive surface, the sky) goes to its own guide, binding 13 (RGBA16F), and into neither NRD image: it is never demodulated and never denoised, and the compositor adds it back.

[[vk::binding(13, 0)]] [[vk::image_format("rgba16f")]] RWTexture2D<float4> emissionAOV; // the camera ray's emissionshaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:47
// What the camera ray meets emits: never demodulated, never denoised (nrd_compose adds it).shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:526

The NRD split follows the lobes. At the first vertex, light sampling and environment sampling evaluate the BSDF lobe by lobe (`evalLobes`) and send the diffuse part to the diffuse image and the specular part to the specular image. Everything after the first bounce goes whole to the image of the lobe that bounce sampled (`firstLobe`):

if (depth == 0u) { diffuseOut += throughput * fd * ls.radiance * w; specularOut += throughput * fs * ls.radiance * w; }shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:636
if (depth == 0u) { diffuseOut += throughput * fd * le; specularOut += throughput * fs * le; }shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:655

The diffuse radiance is demodulated by the first hit's diffuse albedo (floored at 0.03) and the specular is not, so the compositor multiplies the diffuse back by that albedo, takes the specular as it is, and adds the emission:

const float3 diffDemod = diffuseOut / max(diffAlbedo, float3(0.03));shaders/rt/path_tracer.slang:700
vec3 composed = diffRad * diffAlbedo + specRad + emission;shaders/rt/nrd_compose.comp:36

256 bytes of push constants

The push-constant block is three `mat4`s, two `uvec4`s and two `vec4`s, exactly 256 bytes, and a static assert holds it there. That 256 is a device limit, not a spec floor: core Vulkan only guarantees 128 bytes.

OHAO_ASSERT_GPU_LAYOUT(PTPushConstants, layout::kPTPushConstantsBytes);ohao/render/rt/path_tracer.hpp:425
pc.params = glm::uvec4(m_width, m_height, m_sampleIndex, m_maxBounces);ohao/render/rt/path_tracer_render.cpp:518
Key idea

Every path-traced pixel comes from `path_tracer.slang`: one path a sample, next-event estimation to one light and to the environment, BSDF sampling of OpenPBR's base, every strategy weighted by the power heuristic, Russian roulette from the third vertex. The realtime profile is the same estimator at two bounces, ended in the radiance cache past the first vertex, its frames denoised instead of averaged.

Contracts

  • Both profiles run `PathTracerShaderSet`'s default. A set's entry names must match its SPIR-V (`raygen`, `closestHit`, `anyHit`, `miss`, `cacheUpdate`).
  • A light found by both strategies must be weighted on both sides. A bounce ray meets an emitter only when its mask includes `kTlasMaskEmitter`; a light with no emitter mesh must not be flagged by `LightTable::setEmitterLights`, or its light sampling is halved for a hit that never comes.
  • An emitter instance's `emitterLight.w` is its light's index in the RT light buffer. If the two orders differ, a bounce ray weighs one light's emission against another's density.
  • Emissive surfaces reach the path tracer by BSDF sampling only. Putting proxy lights for them back into the RT buffer counts their light twice.
  • A shadow ray's payload must start with `kHit` set; with the closest hit skipped, nothing else sets it on a hit.
  • The bounce cap lives in `params.w`'s low 16 bits.
  • A path ends in the radiance cache only past its first vertex, only with `kFlagCache` set, and only where the cell's voxel fits the segment and the lobe; the first hit is always shaded, so primary visibility and the denoisers' guides never come from the cache.
  • `cacheUpdate` is a second raygen in the same pipeline and file; a change to the path's sampling or MIS in `raygen` must be made in `cacheUpdate` too, or the cache holds a different estimator's light.
  • `control.y` is the number of frames already in the running mean; the raygen reads the history image only when it is above zero.
  • What the camera ray meets emits goes to the emission guide (binding 13) and to neither NRD image; a compositor that skips binding 13 loses lights, emissive surfaces and the sky.
  • NRD's diffuse image is demodulated by the first hit's diffuse albedo and its specular image is not; `nrd_compose` must multiply back exactly what the tracer divided out.

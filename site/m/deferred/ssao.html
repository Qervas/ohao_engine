A heightfield instead of rays

Ambient occlusion is a visibility integral: over the hemisphere above a shading point, what fraction of directions escape to the sky? Answering it honestly costs rays. SSAO is the answer you get without them — a single-layer heightfield of whatever the camera can see, read out of the G-buffer — and everything on this page is a consequence of trying to read occlusion out of that heightfield.

It is not, in this engine, the occlusion a frame leans on for direct light. RT shadows occlude direct light (CSM where they cannot trace), and RT GI's rays already see the occluders between a surface and its bounce light. What neither touches is the constant ambient term and the image-based environment terms: those are lit from every direction with nothing in the way, and that is the light SSAO darkens. It is opt-in — the pipeline gate defaults false:

if (m_ssaoEnabled && m_ssaoPass) {ohao/render/deferred/post_processing_pipeline.cpp:74

and the callers that open it are `model_viewer`'s deferred path, the `ohao_game` host, a live window session that turned it on, and a capture whose `post` list names `ssao`. When it is off the renderer registers no `ssao` pass and clears the lighting pass's SSAO view, so the frame has neither the cost nor the timing:

if (!ssaoOn && m_lightingPass) m_lightingPass->setSSAOTexture(VK_NULL_HANDLE, VK_NULL_HANDLE);ohao/render/deferred/deferred_renderer.cpp:348

Two dispatches at half resolution

`SSAOPass` is two compute entry points of one Slang module, `compute/ssao.slang` — `occlusion` and `blur` — loaded through the same `rtc` helper the RT techniques use and timed together as `ssao`:

if (!rtc::createComputePipeline(m_device, "compute_ssao.slang.occlusion.spv", "occlusion", m_occlusionSetLayout,ohao/render/deferred/ssao_pass.cpp:72

Both write `R16G16_SFLOAT` images at half the frame's size, rounded up: the occlusion before the blur, and the blurred output lighting reads. Each holds a pair, the occlusion and the pixel's distance from the camera (zero for sky), and the distance is the depth guide for everything downstream.

static constexpr VkFormat kOutputFormat = VK_FORMAT_R16G16_SFLOAT;ohao/render/deferred/ssao_pass.hpp:57

Half-resolution pixel $q$ shades full-resolution pixel $2q$, reading its world position and octahedral normal straight from the G-buffer. Nothing is reconstructed from depth, and position and normal are both world space, so the estimator works in one frame throughout. A pixel whose position is exactly zero in all four channels is sky and writes (1, 0):

const int2 p = min(int2(q * 2u), full - 1);shaders/compute/ssao.slang:64

A horizon search in the tangent plane

The occlusion pass is a horizon search. It builds a tangent basis around the normal and walks `config.x` directions in that plane — eight by default — each marched `config.y` steps (four) across the screen toward the projection of a point `radius` metres away (0.5 m), through the unjittered view-projection:

const float4 clip = mul(pc.viewProj, float4(pos + dir * radius, 1.0));shaders/compute/ssao.slang:89

Every sample is a full-resolution G-buffer position. Seen from the shaded point lifted `bias` along its normal (0.025 m), its elevation is the sine of its angle above the tangent plane; a direction keeps its highest elevation, faded by the sample's distance within the radius:

best = max(best, elevation * (1.0 - l2 / (radius * radius)));shaders/compute/ssao.slang:104

With $D$ directions, $s$ the samples of direction $d$, $\theta_s$ a sample's elevation above the tangent plane, $\ell_s$ its distance from the lifted origin, $r$ the radius and $k$ the intensity, the pixel's ambient visibility is

$$ \mathrm{AO} = \operatorname{saturate}\!\left(1 - \frac{k}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D} \max_{s \in d}\; \sin\theta_s \left(1 - \frac{\ell_s^2}{r^2}\right)\right) $$

A direction with nothing above the tangent plane contributes zero, so a flat, unoccluded floor comes out exactly 1 — the property the old view-space estimator lacked. Samples outside the radius, off the screen, on the sky or on the pixel itself are skipped, and a direction whose end point falls behind the camera is skipped whole while still counting in $D$. The result then fades back to 1 between 50 and 300 m from the camera (`falloffStart`, `falloffEnd`), where a 0.5 m radius covers less than a pixel:

ao = lerp(1.0, ao, 1.0 - smoothstep(pc.params.z, pc.params.w, dist));shaders/compute/ssao.slang:109

The lifted origin is what `bias` is for: samples of the shaded surface itself, which sit in the tangent plane, end up slightly *below* the lifted point and so add nothing, instead of registering as a grazing occluder through half-float position error.

Sixteen rotations, every texel used

Eight fixed azimuths band. Each pixel therefore rotates its directions by a fraction of the angle between two of them, taken from a 4×4 interleaved pattern indexed by the half-resolution pixel, texel $(q_x \bmod 4,\, q_y \bmod 4)$:

const uint k = (q.x & 3u) | ((q.y & 3u) << 2u);shaders/compute/ssao.slang:45

The sixteen rotations are a permutation of the sixteen strata $(k + 0.5)/16$, so any 4×4 window holds sixteen evenly spaced rotations. The permutation is a Fisher–Yates shuffle driven by a counter hash of the render seed (`VulkanRenderer::setRenderSeed`, default 0) — integer arithmetic and exact floats, the same on every compiler and machine:

for (uint32_t i = 15; i > 0; --i) std::swap(order[i], order[next() % (i + 1)]);ohao/render/deferred/ssao_pass.cpp:192

They travel in the push constants (`SSAOPush`, defined once in `shaders/shared/layout.json`), not in a noise texture, so reseeding takes effect on the next frame and uploads nothing. Each rotation is its stratum index, four bits, so the sixteen fit in two uints and the block in the 128 bytes every device guarantees:

for (uint32_t i = 0; i < 16; ++i) packed[i / 8] |= static_cast<uint32_t>(n[i] * 16.0f) << ((i % 8) * 4);ohao/render/deferred/ssao_pass.cpp:123

`deferred_determinism_gpu_test` pins the bytes of `SSAOPass::noise(0)` and checks that the sixteen values are the sixteen strata.

The blur, and the upsample in lighting

The `blur` entry averages a 4×4 window, offsets −1 to 2: exactly one period of the noise, so each window holds each of the sixteen rotations once and the interleaving averages out. A tap's weight falls linearly with its distance difference from the centre, to zero at 5 % of the centre's distance; sky taps are skipped, and the centre always counts, so the sum is never empty:

const float w = saturate(1.0 - abs(v.y - c.y) * invTol);shaders/compute/ssao.slang:143

The lighting pass reads the blurred image at binding 10 and raises flag bit 1 (the value 2) when it holds one. Each full-resolution pixel takes the four nearest half-resolution samples, weighted bilinearly and by the same 5 % distance match; where none is the same surface it takes the closest instead of blurring across the edge:

return wsum > 1e-3 ? sum / wsum : best;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:116

What it darkens

The upsampled value multiplies only the light that arrives from everywhere: the constant ambient term and, with an environment map, the IBL terms. Direct light is occluded by RT shadows or CSM, and RT GI's rays already see what is in the way, so neither is touched:

vec3 ambient = vec3(pc.ambientIntensity) * albedo * ao * ssao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:297
ambient += (specularAmbient + (rtGI ? vec3(0.0) : diffuseAmbient)) * ao * ssao;shaders/core/deferred_lighting.frag:348

`ao` there is the G-buffer's baked occlusion (albedo alpha: 1, or the red channel of an occlusion-roughness-metallic texture); `ssao` multiplies on top of it. Test 5 in `engine_tests` checks both halves: with only ambient light, a crease frame has 3191 pixels darker with SSAO on and none brighter; lit by a point light with zero ambient, the frame is unchanged (max_abs 0). A second test renders an SSAO frame from two fresh renderers and finds them bit-identical.

Cost

Measured with `render_bench` on an RTX 5070 Laptop GPU (2026-10-25, a run flagged noisy by outside load), `ssao` on `helmet_game_1080p` took 0.165 ms; the full-resolution pass it replaced took 0.817 ms. Both dispatches are 8×8 workgroups over the half-resolution image: at most 8 × 4 = 32 position fetches a pixel for the occlusion, 16 for the blur.

Sharp edges

Sky is detected by exact zero in all four channels of the G-buffer position, the fourth being metallic, so a non-metallic surface passing exactly through the world origin is sky to SSAO as it is to every other reader.

The radius, bias and falloff are absolute metres: a 0.5 m radius is a crease detail in a room and invisible in a canyon. `setRadius`, `setBias` and `setIntensity` exist, and `PostProcessingPipeline` forwards radius and intensity, but nothing in the tree calls them, so every frame runs the defaults.

It is still a heightfield. A sample within the radius counts by its elevation alone, with no thickness test, so a thin object in front of a wall occludes the wall as if it were solid to the depth buffer's far side; and anything off screen or behind the first surface does not exist.

The pass has two entry points in one module and is not hot-reloadable: `reloadShader` logs and returns false.

Key idea

SSAO is now a consumer-visible term, and a narrow one: a half-resolution horizon search in world space, sixteen seeded rotations from the push constants, a one-period blur, and a depth-aware upsample into the ambient and IBL terms only. Nothing it computes can darken direct light or RT GI, and a frame with neither ambient nor environment light is bit-identical with it on or off.

Why

The images are kept outside the render graph's barrier ledger: the pass transitions both itself, discarding last frame's contents on the way to `GENERAL` and ending in `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL` for the compute and fragment readers. {{cite ohao/render/deferred/ssao_pass.cpp "rtc::transition(cmd, m_output, kReadOnly, kCompute, VK_ACCESS_SHADER_WRITE_BIT, kReaders, VK_ACCESS_SHADER_READ_BIT, false);"}} The graph declares the pass's G-buffer normal read and nothing else — not the position read, not either image — so the pass is drop-in for any caller with a command buffer. The price is that the ledger cannot check it. Discarding each frame also means no frame amortises its taps against the last; the blur, not history, removes the noise, so the result is the same in every frame of a still camera.

Contracts

  • `execute()` does nothing until `setInputs()` has the G-buffer's position and normal views; `DeferredRenderer::render` hands them, with the unjittered view-projection and the camera position, through `PostProcessingPipeline::setSSAOInputs` every frame before the graph runs. Both inputs must be in `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL` when the pass executes, which the G-buffer render pass leaves them in.
  • The output is half resolution, rounded up, `(ao, distance from the camera)`, zero distance for sky. Any reader must upsample it against a full-resolution distance and treat distance zero as no sample, as `ssaoAt` does.
  • Lighting applies SSAO only to the constant ambient and IBL terms. A new ambient-like term belongs inside that product; direct light and RT GI must stay outside it, or occlusion is counted twice.
  • The noise is a function of the render seed alone. Anything that changes `SSAOPass::noise` changes every SSAO frame and must update the pin in `deferred_determinism_gpu_test`.
  • Descriptor sets are written at record time, one pair per frame in flight, so `frameIndex` must be the frame-ring slot whose fence was just waited on.
  • `onResize` waits for the device idle and recreates both images; the descriptor sets are rewritten every frame, so nothing else needs refreshing.
